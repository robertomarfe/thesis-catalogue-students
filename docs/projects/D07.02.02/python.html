D07.02.02 | Student research starter

Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

Gaussian and square-root models can agree on today’s curve and one liquid at-the-money bond option while implying different tails and sensitivities. A controlled comparison isolates those distributional implications rather than confounding them with different fitted term structures.

Research question: After matching initial bond prices and at-the-money option volatility approximately, how do Gaussian and square-root dynamics affect out-of-the-money bond options and their Greeks?

Student-owned research

Freeze the ATM match before comparing other strikes; report domain and uncertainty rather than forcing implied volatilities for zero prices. Distinct from A05.01.01 physical transition estimation.

  1. Derive bond prices and shift, verify initial curve and forward option parity.

  2. Implement Gaussian analytic and CIR reference pricers, compare MC with time-step refinement.

  3. Match ATM price by one volatility parameter; report unmatched cases.

  4. Compare price/forward-moneyness grids, implied Black vol where defined and finite-difference rate sensitivities.

  5. Stress low initial rates, negative shifted rates and large volatility; keep expiry/delivery fixed. Compare parallel initial-zero-curve sensitivities for at least one ATM and one non-negligible OTM option; keep contractual strike and stochastic parameters fixed and refit only the deterministic curve shift. Treat shifted-CIR support restrictions as structural zeros.

Minimum viable thesis: Two affine models on the same synthetic curve, analytic/Monte Carlo cross-check, one ATM price match and option smile comparison; compare at least one ATM and one non-negligible OTM parallel zero-yield sensitivity with strike and stochastic parameters frozen and deterministic curve fitting updated. Report convergence in bump size, pricing-domain failures and unmatched cases. Distinct from A05.01.01 (physical rate dynamics).

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
