D07.02.02 · Derivatives · Fixed income & interest rates

Interest Rate Options under Gaussian and Square-Root Dynamics

Research question

After matching initial bond prices and at-the-money option volatility approximately, how do Gaussian and square-root dynamics affect out-of-the-money bond options and their Greeks?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Shift and support

For unshifted positive factor xx with discount curve PxP_x, set Pr(0,T)=Px(0,T)exp(−∫0Tφ(s)ds)P_r(0,T)=P_x(0,T)\exp(-\int_0^T\varphi(s)ds). The shift is determined by the ratio of the target and base discount curves. The shifted short rate can be negative; the CIR state remains nonnegative. At expiry uu, shifted bond price is Px(u,T)exp(−∫uTφ(s)ds)P_x(u,T)\exp(-\int_u^T\varphi(s)ds). Because the CIR bond price is decreasing in the factor, it has a finite upper endpoint. A strike above that endpoint produces a structural zero, not a numerical failure.

Matched comparisons

Use the same initial forward and strike; one ATM match does not imply equality of other prices. Validate affine pricing against exact-transition CIR simulation with refined integrated discounting, and an independent Gaussian benchmark. For parallel curve bumps re-fit the deterministic shift but keep stochastic dynamics and strike fixed. Report unmatched cases, OTM structural zeros, and a non-negligible OTM sensitivity with bump convergence.

Research checks

Freeze the ATM match before comparing other strikes; report domain and uncertainty rather than forcing implied volatilities for zero prices. Distinct from A05.01.01 physical transition estimation.
