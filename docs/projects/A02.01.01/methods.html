Can Valuation Ratios Predict the Equity Premium?

Can lagged dividend-price and earnings-price ratios improve monthly US equity excess-return forecasts over the historical mean, and is their performance stable across decades? Assess whether the gains remain after accounting for parameter uncertainty and timing conventions.

Focused methods / A02.01.01

1. Economic motivation and forecast timing

The log-linear present-value identity relates the dividend-price ratio to discounted future returns and dividend growth, plus a terminal term. It does not establish that either ratio forecasts excess returns in finite samples. Define monthly origins tt and target completion dates e=t+1e=t+1. The conservative admissible estimation set is

ℐt={s:s<t,es<t,xs,ysobserved}.\mathcal I_t=\{s:s<t,\ e_s<t,\ x_s,y_s\ \text{observed}\}.

Use this same set for the mean benchmark and OLS. At a forecast origin, xtx_t must be available; the strict label rule alone does not establish predictor availability. Earnings-release lags require a separate data audit. For a 240-month window add s≥t−240s\geq t-240. Do not replace missing calendar months with adjacent rows.

2. Evaluation

Let yt=rt+1ey_t=r^e_{t+1}, ftf_t be the fitted forecast and btb_t the historical mean. Over the same complete evaluation origins 𝒯\mathcal T,

ROS2=1−∑t∈𝒯(yt−ft)2∑t∈𝒯(yt−bt)2.R^2_{\mathrm{OS}}=1-\frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}(y_t-f_t)^2}{\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}(y_t-b_t)^2}.

The cumulative gain uses dt=(yt−bt)2−(yt−ft)2d_t=(y_t-b_t)^2-(y_t-f_t)^2. Positive values favor the regression. Never use a full-sample mean or standardization. Negative ROS2R^2_{\mathrm{OS}} does not invalidate a research design.

3. Inference and robustness

For nested forecast models the Clark–West adjusted loss differential is

dtCW=dt+(ft−bt)2.d_t^{CW}=d_t+(f_t-b_t)^2.

Distinguish its one-sided comparison from the raw loss differential, an in-sample HAC tt-statistic, and a claim of population economic predictability. A paired date-block bootstrap of fixed forecasts addresses score variability but not all re-estimation uncertainty. Longer-horizon returns require compounding total-return and bill legs separately, and overlapping target dependence.
