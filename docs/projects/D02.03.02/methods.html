Calendar-Spread Arbitrage and the Construction of Volatility Surfaces

Research question

What fit and stability costs arise from enforcing calendar and strike no-arbitrage restrictions on noisy multi-expiry option quotes?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Forward-normalized restrictions

For deterministic rates and proportional dividends, define x=K/FTx=K/F_T, c(x,T)=C(K,T)/(DTFT)c(x,T)=C(K,T)/(D_TF_T) and w(k,T)=TI(k,T)2w(k,T)=T I(k,T)^2, with k=logxk=\log x. The normalized underlying ST/FTS_T/F_T is a martingale under ℚ\mathbb Q. Conditional Jensen gives increasing call expectations in TT at fixed xx, equivalent to nondecreasing ww at fixed kk for Black prices. Raw-strike IV-level monotonicity is not the condition. In strike, require (1−x)+≤c≤1(1-x)^+\le c\le1 and increasing slopes in [−1,0][-1,0].

Joint grid fit

Use a common ordered xx grid. With maturities Ta<TbT_a<T_b, impose cj,b≥cj,ac_{j,b}\ge c_{j,a} at every common node, in addition to per-slice bounds, monotonicity and convexity. Estimate a constrained least-squares grid on observed cells only, preserving masks for missing cells; anchor x=0,c=1x=0,c=1 and explicitly treat a distant finite tail node as an approximation. Linear interpolation in xx and between maturities preserves the finite-domain inequalities on a common grid. Verify them after interpolation and examine sensitivity to the tail choice. Compare accuracy against held-out clean truth separately from objective fit to noisy quotes; no global extrapolation or smooth-density claim follows.

Research checks

Use normalized strikes and identical forward inputs before assessing calendars. Distinguish observed masks, withheld truth and artificial tail anchors; check between-node restrictions and report solver slacks and adjustment cost.
