Forecasting Equity Market Volatility: GARCH versus Historical Volatility

Research question

Does GARCH(1,1) improve genuinely out-of-sample forecasts of daily and 21-trading-day market variance relative to rolling historical variance? Is the ranking stable in high- and low-volatility periods?

1. Forecast objects and recursion

Let ℱt\mathcal F_t contain observations through trading day tt. The return innovation utu_t is the log return minus the predetermined mean. For GARCH(1,1), write ht|t−1h_{t|t-1} for the one-step conditional variance used when evaluating utu_t. Gaussian QML minimizes the mean of

ℓt(ω,α,β)=12[loght|t−1+ut2/ht|t−1]\ell_t(\omega,\alpha,\beta)=\tfrac12\left[\log h_{t|t-1}+u_t^2/h_{t|t-1}\right]
over the current training window. Reinitialize the filter at h‾=ω/(1−α−β)\bar h=\omega/(1-\alpha-\beta) under the stated initialization convention and disclose the initialization. Require positive variance, nonnegative coefficients and persistence strictly below one. The fitted parameters are held fixed between declared refit dates, but the conditional variance incorporates each newly observed innovation.

At origin tt the first forecast is ht+1|t=ω+αut2+βht|t−1h_{t+1|t}=\omega+\alpha u_t^2+\beta h_{t|t-1}. Define p=α+βp=\alpha+\beta. The jj-step forecast and aggregate forecast are

ht+j|t=h‾+pj−1(ht+1|t−h‾),h_{t+j|t}=\bar h+p^{j-1}(h_{t+1|t}-\bar h),
ft,H=∑j=1Hht+j|t=Hh‾+(ht+1|t−h‾)1−pH1−p.f_{t,H}=\sum_{j=1}^H h_{t+j|t}=H\bar h+(h_{t+1|t}-\bar h)\frac{1-p^H}{1-p}.
For a rolling historical window of length ww, use
ft,Hhist=Hw∑i=0w−1ut−i2.f^{\mathrm{hist}}_{t,H}=\frac{H}{w}\sum_{i=0}^{w-1}u_{t-i}^2.
Both are forecasts of a sum in daily variance units. Do not compare a daily forecast with annualized volatility, or 21 trading days with exactly 30 calendar days. A constant-variance benchmark can use a fixed training estimate.

2. Information and target maturation

For each origin tt record the training end date, refit identifier, horizon HH, prediction and target end date t+Ht+H. The evaluation proxy is Yt,H=∑j=1Hut+j2Y_{t,H}=\sum_{j=1}^H u_{t+j}^2; it is unavailable until t+Ht+H. Retain only common, mature outcomes for all compared forecasts. To set regimes or tune parameters, use information already available at the forecast origin, never the realized future proxy. Nonoverlapping 21-day origins address a separate dependence issue; they do not remove all serial dependence.

3. Forecast comparison

For positive forecast ff and nonnegative proxy YY, define

LQL(Y,f)=logf+Y/f,LMSE(Y,f)=(Y−f)2.L_{QL}(Y,f)=\log f+Y/f,\qquad L_{MSE}(Y,f)=(Y-f)^2.
QLIKE may be negative because of the log term. Smaller loss is better. Compare on identical origins within each horizon; the level of QLIKE is not comparable across differently scaled targets. The validity of relative loss comparisons with imperfect proxies requires suitable conditional unbiasedness assumptions. For loss difference dt=LQL(Yt,ftG)−LQL(Yt,ftH)d_t=L_{QL}(Y_t,f_t^G)-L_{QL}(Y_t,f_t^H), negative mean favors GARCH. Use HAC or a block bootstrap to account for serial dependence (especially overlapping 21-day targets); declare bandwidth or block size and report uncertainty. The pilot’s small artificial sample is not an inferential demonstration.
