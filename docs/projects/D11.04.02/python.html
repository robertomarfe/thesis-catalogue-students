D11.04.02 | Student research starter

Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

Incomplete markets allow a claim to have different values to buyers and sellers who cannot trade away its residual risk. Exponential utility isolates the effect of risk aversion and the hedgeable component in a one-period economy, providing an economically interpretable comparison with actuarial and replication benchmarks.

Research question: How do the unhedgeable component and risk aversion determine the utility indifference price relative to complete-market and actuarial benchmarks?

Student-owned research

Compare numerical certainty-equivalent equality and analytic prices, risk-aversion and correlation sensitivity and scaling. If a nonlinear claim is added, distinguish a preference-dependent value from no-arbitrage bounds.

  1. Derive Gaussian indifference price, buyer-seller spread and hedge.

  2. Implement independent numerical utility optimization against closed form.

  3. Vary correlation, gamma and market Sharpe ratio with common inputs.

  4. Compare nonlinear finite-state prices to bounds and minimum-variance hedge, check no-arbitrage and replicability limits.

  5. Report sensitivity to payoff scale and physical probabilities, not a unique market price.

Minimum viable thesis: Gaussian derivation and independent optimizer, replicability limit, risk-aversion and correlation sensitivity, one nonlinear finite-state claim if time permits.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
