Gaussian certainty equivalent

Let U(w)=−e−γwU(w)=-e^{-\gamma w}, γ>0\gamma>0. For Gaussian wealth ZZ, the certainty equivalent is 𝔼Z−γVar(Z)/2\mathbb E Z-\gamma\operatorname{Var}(Z)/2. With zero-cost traded gain GG and nontraded claim HH, write b=Cov(H,G)/Var(G)b=\operatorname{Cov}(H,G)/\operatorname{Var}(G) and s2=Var(H)−b2Var(G)s^2=\operatorname{Var}(H)-b^2\operatorname{Var}(G). Maximizing the certainty equivalent without the claim gives position 𝔼G/(γVarG)\mathbb E G/(\gamma\operatorname{Var}G); selling the claim adds bb. The resulting seller price is psell=𝔼H−b𝔼G+γs2/2.p_{sell}=\mathbb E H-b\mathbb E G+\gamma s^2/2. The buyer valuation subtracts the risk charge. For H=c+bGH=c+bG, both equal cc. In the non-Gaussian finite-state extension, compute expected utility via a stable log-sum-exp and verify optimizer conditions; do not impose the Gaussian formula.

Interpretation

All values are discounted and γ\gamma has inverse wealth units. Changing notional changes the unhedgeable risk charge nonlinearly. Physical probabilities and the chosen utility are maintained assumptions, not a unique pricing measure.

Research checks

Compare numerical certainty-equivalent equality and analytic prices, risk-aversion and correlation sensitivity and scaling. If a nonlinear claim is added, distinguish a preference-dependent value from no-arbitrage bounds.
