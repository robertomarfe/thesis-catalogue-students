Derivatives · Credit risk and incomplete markets · Level 3/3

Data: Synthetic core; no mandatory external data. Status: current review draft; supervisor approval pending.

Motivation

Incomplete markets allow a claim to have different values to buyers and sellers who cannot trade away its residual risk. Exponential utility isolates the effect of risk aversion and the hedgeable component in a one-period economy, providing an economically interpretable comparison with actuarial and replication benchmarks.

Research question

How do the unhedgeable component and risk aversion determine the utility indifference price relative to complete-market and actuarial benchmarks?

Economic mechanism

Research design

Use the prescribed joint Gaussian discounted gain and claim, correlation and risk-aversion grids. Compare analytical and independent numerical certainty equivalents, and examine the replicable limit. A nonlinear finite-state claim is an extension when feasible.

Compare numerical certainty-equivalent equality and analytic prices, risk-aversion and correlation sensitivity and scaling. If a nonlinear claim is added, distinguish a preference-dependent value from no-arbitrage bounds.

Required exhibits

Price and hedge surfaces, buyer-seller spread, exact-versus-numeric residuals, payoff-state diagram, sensitivity to nontraded risk.

Minimum viable thesis

Gaussian derivation and independent optimizer, replicability limit, risk-aversion and correlation sensitivity, one nonlinear finite-state claim if time permits.

Scope and feasibility

Do not treat P as Q; utility prices can differ across agents and order sizes. Use discounted payoffs consistently; exclude position constraints from the analytic benchmark. Utility specification and endowment are maintained assumptions.

Prerequisites: Expected utility, asset pricing, Gaussian moments, convex optimization.

Optional extension: Study random initial endowment and the interaction with the nontraded claim using joint finite-state probabilities.

Milestones

W1-3: derivation; W4-6: certainty-equivalent and optimizer tests; W7-9: sensitivities; W10-12: finite-state case; W13-16: writing.
