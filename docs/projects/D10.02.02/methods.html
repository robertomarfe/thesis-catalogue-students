Pricing Commodity Options under Mean Reversion

How does mean reversion affect the maturity pattern of option prices on commodity forwards, holding the initial forward curve fixed?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Forward dynamics and total variance

In a one-factor Gaussian log-price model under pricing probabilities, deterministic curve fitting gives a forward with loading σe−κ(T−t)\sigma e^{-\kappa(T-t)} and zero drift under deterministic rates. For expiry u≤Tu\le T its log variance is v(u,T)=σ22κ(e−2κ(T−u)−e−2κT).v(u,T)=\frac{\sigma^2}{2\kappa}\left(e^{-2\kappa(T-u)}-e^{-2\kappa T}\right). As κ→0\kappa\to0, v→σ2uv\to\sigma^2u. The exact log-forward transition has mean logF(0,T)−v/2\log F(0,T)-v/2 and variance vv, ensuring the forward martingale. Distinguish uu from TT and keep the initial forward fixed across models.

Option valuation and matching

With deterministic rr, use Black-76 e−ru[F(0,T)Φ(d1)−KΦ(d2)]e^{-ru}[F(0,T)\Phi(d_1)-K\Phi(d_2)] for a call, with d1=(log(F/K)+v/2)/vd_1=(\log(F/K)+v/2)/\sqrt v and d2=d1−vd_2=d_1-\sqrt v. Compare matched instantaneous volatility and matched reference total variance at u=T=1u=T=1; the second requires rescaling σ\sigma with κ\kappa. Check the martingale, independent payoff simulation, confidence intervals and delivery-lag sensitivity. The model assumes positive prices and is not fitted to observed oil options.

Research checks

Check the constant-volatility limit, separate expiry from delivery and hold forward/discount fixed. Use independent seeds, report sampling error and keep both matching designs distinct.
