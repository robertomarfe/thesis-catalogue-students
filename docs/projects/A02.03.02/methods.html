Structural Instability in Equity Premium Predictability

Do rolling or discounted-window predictive regressions outperform expanding estimates when equity premium predictability is unstable?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

At origin tt, solve mina,b∑s∈𝒯tws,t(ys+1−a−bxs)2\min_{a,b}\sum_{s\in\mathcal T_t}w_{s,t}(y_{s+1}-a-bx_s)^2 using matured targets. Expanding uses all eligible history, rolling uses a fixed last WW observations, and discounted uses ws,t=2−(t−s)/Hw_{s,t}=2^{-(t-s)/H} with prespecified half-life HH. In simulation, contrast a stable slope with a fixed known change in btb_t under common innovation draws. A hindsight break estimate may describe the sample but cannot select a forecast rule on the final block.

Identification and implementation

For fixed design and slope drift, weighted least squares estimates a weighted projection of the history, not automatically the current slope. Shorter windows lower stale-data bias after a break but increase estimation variance. Report adaptation delay under common simulated paths and separate retrospective sup-Wald diagnostics from executable forecasts.

Research checks

Hand-check weighted least squares on a toy matrix; rolling window never uses an immature label; freeze menu before holdout; report MC uncertainty and stable-null behavior; do not optimize break location on final errors.
