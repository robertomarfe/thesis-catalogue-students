Expected Economic Growth and the Dynamics of Equity Risk Premia

Do published US real GDP growth forecasts predict future equity excess returns beyond valuation ratios, and does incremental predictive performance survive chronological evaluation?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

For survey vintage qq, define expected growth from mean real GDP levels EGq=100[(FqYq+1/FqYq)4−1]EG_q=100[(F_qY_{q+1}/F_qY_q)^4-1].

This is annualized growth of mean forecast levels, not mean individual growth. At month-end origin tt after release, define yt,h=∑j=1h(logRm,t+j−logRb,t+j)y_{t,h}=\sum_{j=1}^h(\log R_{m,t+j}-\log R_{b,t+j}), where the gross market and bill legs are reconstructed separately. Fit yu,h=a+bEGu+cdpu+eu,hy_{u,h}=a+b EG_u+c dp_u+e_{u,h} only on origins whose targets end strictly before tt. Compare EG-only, dp-only, joint OLS and mature historical mean on identical origins. Positive out-of-sample R2R^2 is not causal evidence. Three- and twelve-month targets overlap; use paired calendar blocks with model refits. Revised data without verified release vintages imply pseudo-real-time evaluation.

Research checks

Create an origin/release/target-end ledger; reconstruct first three forecasts from the actual workbook, and verify mature training targets at each decision. Compare EG, dp and both on identical origins with a mature historical mean; report effective sample, overlapping h=12 sensitivity, paired loss intervals and pseudo-real-time limitation.
