Sparse Predictors and the Factor Zoo

Research question

Do sparse factor selections learned on one set of test assets and historical dates reduce pricing errors for different assets on future dates relative to FF3/FF5 and dense shrinkage?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

For centered factor vector f−μff-\mu_f, the normalized linear kernel m=1−b⊤(f−μf)m=1-b^\top(f-\mu_f) gives excess-payoff pricing moment g(b)=E[re]−Cov(re,f)bg(b)=E[r^e]-\operatorname{Cov}(r^e,f)b on the training sample.

Identification and implementation

Let BB be the training covariance matrix with factors standardized using training scale. Minimize 12g(b)⊤Wg(b)+α∑j∈𝒫|bj|\frac12 g(b)^\top Wg(b)+\alpha\sum_{j\in\mathcal P}|b_j| with a common SPD training weight WW and unpenalized FF3 terms. For active penalized coefficients, Bj⊤Wg=αsign(bj)B_j^\top Wg=\alpha\operatorname{sign}(b_j); inactive terms satisfy absolute score at most α\alpha; core scores vanish. On held-out data compute Etest[mre]E_{test}[m r^e] with frozen training centering, not a re-centered test covariance. The kernel is not guaranteed positive or gross-payoff normalized.

Research checks

Use one shared OSAP revision and distinct file hashes; freeze candidate selection and common SPD weight on training only. Check KKT, scale and unpenalized FF3. Compare same assets future and different assets future, fixed kernel, mean and scale, common metric. No post-selection p-values. Conditional access; no silent substitution.
