D09.04.01 · Derivatives · Credit & incomplete markets

Counterparty Credit Risk and the Pricing of Uncollateralized Derivatives

How do maturity, market volatility, hazard and recovery jointly shape unilateral CVA for an uncollateralized forward, and how sensitive is independence-based CVA to wrong-way risk?

Synthetic core · Level 2/3 · Research draft

Synthetic core. No external dataset required for the core design; optional extensions may require data.

OverviewResearch proposalTheory & methodsLiteratureData & sourcesPython labResearch plan

Research overview

Motivation

A forward contract can be fairly priced at inception yet expose its holder to a loss if the counterparty defaults before settlement. The potential loss depends on the future replacement value at the time of default, not on the contractual notional alone. A useful valuation adjustment therefore requires both a model of market risk and a model of default. This project quantifies their joint importance, and asks whether an independence approximation remains informative when default intensity responds to the underlying price.

Minimum viable thesis

One equity forward, independent default, analytic exposure and independent Monte Carlo check, four hazard/volatility scenarios; at one maturity, compare independent and two signed adapted Cox-intensity cases after matching the marginal survival curve on a common grid. Report default probabilities, CVA, simulation and time-grid uncertainty and the separate fixed-baseline comparison.

Prerequisites

BSM, credit intensity, numerical integration, Python.

Methods

Pricing & no-arbitrage · Stochastic processes · Simulation & Monte Carlo · Fixed income & curves · Tail risk & distributions
