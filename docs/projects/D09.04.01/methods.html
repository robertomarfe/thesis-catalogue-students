1. Discounted exposure

For a long forward on a stock paying a continuous dividend yield qq, let a=S0e−qTa=S_0e^{-qT} and b=Ke−rTb=Ke^{-rT}. The discounted replacement value at time tt is

e−rtVt=aexp(−12σ2t+σWt)−b.e^{-rt}V_t=a\exp(-\tfrac12\sigma^2t+\sigma W_t)-b.

Thus E(t)=𝔼ℚ[e−rtVt+]=aΦ(d1)−bΦ(d2)E(t)=\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q}[e^{-rt}V_t^+]=a\Phi(d_1)-b\Phi(d_2), where Φ\Phi denotes the standard normal CDF and

d1=log(a/b)+σ2t/2σt,d2=d1−σt.d_1=\frac{\log(a/b)+\sigma^2t/2}{\sigma\sqrt t},\qquad d_2=d_1-\sigma\sqrt t.

At t=0t=0 or σ=0\sigma=0, use (a−b)+(a-b)^+. The exposure defined here is discounted: do not discount it again. With independent exponential default of intensity hh, the continuous benchmark is

C=(1−R)∫0TE(t)he−htdt.C=(1-R)\int_0^T E(t)he^{-ht}\,dt.

It is nonnegative and vanishes if R=1R=1 or h=0h=0. An ATM forward with σ=0\sigma=0 has zero exposure under this specification. The upper-endpoint grid approximation at dates tjt_j is

Cn=(1−R)∑j=1nE(tj)[e−htj−1−e−htj].C_n=(1-R)\sum_{j=1}^n E(t_j)[e^{-ht_{j-1}}-e^{-ht_j}].

The difference Cn−CC_n-C reflects time discretization, not Monte Carlo sampling error. Simulating an independent default clock permits a second estimator; the exact-time exposure benchmark avoids the endpoint approximation. Recovery here applies to positive replacement value, not to notional or a debt face value.

2. Adapted Cox intensity

Let ℓj=log(Stj/S0)\ell_j=\log(S_{t_j}/S_0), ℓ0=0\ell_0=0, and Δj=tj−tj−1\Delta_j=t_j-t_{j-1}. On interval jj, fix the intensity at λj=bjexp(βℓj−1)\lambda_j=b_j\exp(\beta\ell_{j-1}). It is predictable at the start of the interval. An exponential random variable of mean one, independent of market shocks, supplies the Cox default clock. Conditional survival at node jj is

Gj=exp{−∑i=1jλiΔi},G0=1.G_j=\exp\{-\sum_{i=1}^j\lambda_i\Delta_i\},\qquad G_0=1.

3. Matching the marginal credit curve

On calibration paths indexed by cc, solve sequentially for nonnegative bjb_j from

1Nc∑cGj−1(c)e−bjeβℓj−1(c)Δj=e−htj.\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_c G_{j-1}^{(c)}e^{-b_j e^{\beta\ell_{j-1}^{(c)}}\Delta_j}=e^{-ht_j}.

The left side decreases continuously in bjb_j for finite positive intensities and positive previous weights. Its value at zero equals the preceding fitted survival; its limit at infinity is zero. A bracketed scalar root gives a unique solution for a strictly decreasing target. Reuse the fitted baselines on independent evaluation paths, not the paths used to fit them. For a fixed-baseline comparison set bj=hb_j=h without matching. The two comparisons answer different economic questions.

4. Conditional estimator and diagnostics

With endpoint exposure ej=e−rtjVtj+e_j=e^{-rt_j}V_{t_j}^+, the conditional-on-market-path loss is

L=(1−R)∑j=1nej(Gj−1−Gj).L=(1-R)\sum_{j=1}^n e_j(G_{j-1}-G_j).

Average LL on independent evaluation paths; the default probability estimate is 1−G¯n1-\overline G_n. The conditional estimator integrates out the default clock. A separate exponential-clock draw, first crossing and endpoint payoff gives an independent conditional sampling check, using the same exposure convention. Neither estimator is a continuous-time benchmark at finite grid size.

Report the maximum survival error across all nodes, evaluation-sample standard errors conditional on fitted baselines, paired changes in CVA and uncertainty from independent calibration replications. When the sign of β\beta changes, the effect is not inferred from the sign alone: it depends on the exposure, the marginal matching and the path dynamics.
