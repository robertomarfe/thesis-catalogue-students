Goal

A euro investor holding US equities is exposed both to equity performance and to movements in the euro-dollar exchange rate. A principal hedge does not automatically remove FX exposure from subsequent equity gains, and the optimal hedge depends on covariance and financing carry.

Research question: When does a quarterly synthetic currency hedge improve the risk-return profile of a euro investor holding US equities? How do hedge ratio, equity-FX covariance and financing carry affect the result?

Student-owned research

Audit USD/EUR sign, full-principal identity, common dates, lagged rates and no look-ahead. Retain 60 aligned-quarter target; if unavailable agree a descriptive fixed-ratio scope. No ex-post optimized benchmark.

  1. Validate currency orientation, compounding and terminal hedge P&L with constant equity/FX examples.

  2. Construct unhedged and hedged euro quarterly returns; report mean, volatility, downside risk and drawdown, with carry and residual equity-FX exposure separately.

  3. Estimate expanding minimum-variance hedge ratio on past quarterly observations only; compare fixed ratios in common test dates.

  4. Scenario-test hypothetical 5-20 bp one-way notional costs and 25-100 bp annual basis shifts; report turnover/rebalancing conventions and results across subsamples.

Minimum viable thesis: At least 60 aligned quarters if available, correctly signed forward P&L, fixed hedge ratios and an expanding benchmark, risk/return comparisons and transparent basis/cost scenarios. If data insufficient, fixed-ratio descriptive exercise with no dynamic forecast claim.
