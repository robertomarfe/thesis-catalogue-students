Commodity Price Risk and the Minimum-Variance Hedge Ratio

How much does a Brent spot-proxy hedge reduce WTI price risk, and how stable is the estimated minimum-variance hedge ratio across horizons and market regimes?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Minimum-variance proxy

For a fixed long exposure of one WTI barrel and a short hh Brent barrels, the change in matched value is ΔV=ΔSW−hΔSB\Delta V=\Delta S_W-h\Delta S_B. With positive Brent-change variance the unconstrained population ratio is h*=Cov(ΔSW,ΔSB)Var(ΔSB).h^*=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(\Delta S_W,\Delta S_B)}{\operatorname{Var}(\Delta S_B)}. Estimate using only completed intervals before the evaluation interval; report near-zero denominator cases, allowed constraints and resulting notional. The unit-hedge residual is the change in the spot price basis. Compare variance reduction 1−Var̂(ΔV)/Var̂(ΔSW)1-\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\Delta V)/\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\Delta S_W) on common test dates; it may be negative.

Price observations and limits

Use dollar changes, not log returns, as baseline, so nonpositive prices are not silently deleted. Audit different holidays, fixing times and endpoint gaps. Weekly and monthly horizons have different risk units and sample sizes. Use paired block intervals and regime counts. This design omits futures contract roll, margin and financing; it does not establish executable hedge performance.

Research checks

Verify common observed endpoints, barrel units, no forward fill and past-only estimation at both horizons. Report basis changes, held-out risk reduction and negative results.
