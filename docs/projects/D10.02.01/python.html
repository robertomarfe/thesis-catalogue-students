Goal

WTI and Brent price changes often co-move, but regional supply and delivery conditions can create substantial basis risk. A statistical Brent spot proxy may reduce WTI exposure; it is not a claim about a self-financing futures hedge.

Research question: How much does a Brent spot-proxy hedge reduce WTI price risk, and how stable is the estimated minimum-variance hedge ratio across horizons and market regimes?

Student-owned research

Verify common observed endpoints, barrel units, no forward fill and past-only estimation at both horizons. Report basis changes, held-out risk reduction and negative results.

  1. Align prices, inspect nonpositive quotes and gaps, plot WTI-Brent basis and correlation.

  2. Derive the hedge ratio in dollars per barrel and test zero and unit-ratio benchmarks.

  3. Estimate rolling covariance ratios from past observations only; compare unrestricted and clipped [0,2] ratios.

  4. Evaluate OOS mean, SD, variance reduction, downside quantiles and hedge-ratio turnover at each horizon.

  5. Assess crisis sensitivity, including 2020, with paired block bootstrap and no deletion of unfavorable observations from the baseline.

Minimum viable thesis: Two spot series, two horizons, rolling minimum-variance and unit hedge benchmarks, honest held-out variance reduction and basis diagnostics.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
