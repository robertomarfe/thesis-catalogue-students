D09.01.01 · Derivatives · Credit & incomplete markets

The Merton Model and the Economics of Corporate Default

Research question

How do leverage, asset volatility and debt maturity jointly determine physical default probabilities, risk-neutral debt values and equity leverage? When do default probability and credit spread move differently?

Terminal claims and probabilities

Under ℚ\mathbb Q, assume dV/V=rdt+σVdWℚdV/V=r\,dt+\sigma_V\,dW^{\mathbb Q}, no payouts and deterministic rr. Define

d1ℚ=log(V0/F)+(r+σV2/2)TσVT,d2ℚ=d1ℚ−σVT.d_1^\mathbb Q=\frac{\log(V_0/F)+(r+\sigma_V^2/2)T}{\sigma_V\sqrt T},\quad d_2^\mathbb Q=d_1^\mathbb Q-\sigma_V\sqrt T.
The call value is E0=V0Φ(d1ℚ)−Fe−rTΦ(d2ℚ)E_0=V_0\Phi(d_1^\mathbb Q)-Fe^{-rT}\Phi(d_2^\mathbb Q); debt is V0−E0V_0-E_0. Here Φ\Phi is the standard normal CDF. Equivalently debt equals the risk-free promise minus a put, which is numerically useful in the far tail. Default probability is Φ(−d2ℚ)\Phi(-d_2^\mathbb Q) under pricing probabilities. Under ℙ\mathbb P use the asset drift μ\mu instead of rr in d2d_2; do not use physical default probability in the debt pricing formula.

Equity leverage and credit spread

Ito differentiation gives σE=(V0/E0)Φ(d1ℚ)σV\sigma_E=(V_0/E_0)\Phi(d_1^\mathbb Q)\sigma_V. The promised yield is the yield on the contractual face amount, not expected realized return. Compare both default frequency and conditional loss, with a numerically stable log loss ratio when debt is nearly risk-free.

Research checks

Verify asset-value conservation, the upper bound on debt and probability intervals. Report simulation uncertainty in rare-default cases and physical-drift sensitivity separately from risk-neutral pricing. Keep all other parameters fixed in each comparative static.
