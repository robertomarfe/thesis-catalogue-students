D09.01.01 | Student research starter

Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

Leverage gives creditors a nonlinear claim on firm assets. The same probability of default need not imply the same borrowing cost, because severity and the price of risk enter debt valuation. Study these distinctions in a transparent terminal-default economy before introducing contractual complexity.

Research question: How do leverage, asset volatility and debt maturity jointly determine physical default probabilities, risk-neutral debt values and equity leverage? When do default probability and credit spread move differently?

Student-owned research

Verify asset-value conservation, the upper bound on debt and probability intervals. Report simulation uncertainty in rare-default cases and physical-drift sensitivity separately from risk-neutral pricing. Keep all other parameters fixed in each comparative static.

  1. Derive prices, both default probabilities and equity elasticity; implement analytical and MC checks.

  2. Map leverage-volatility grids for s, PD_P, PD_Q and sigma_E by maturity; quantify economic sensitivities in bp and percentage points.

  3. Hold PD_P approximately constant across different asset drifts/volatilities using a prespecified grid or root solve; compare resulting spreads and equity volatility.

  4. Discuss why terminal-only default, zero coupons and GBM limit the applicability of very short spreads and realistic credit term structures.

Minimum viable thesis: Correct structural formulas and independent verification, comparative statics on leverage and volatility at several maturities, distinction between P and Q, and quantitative implications. No empirical validation claim is necessary.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
