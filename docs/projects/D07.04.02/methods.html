D07.04.02 · Derivatives · Fixed income & interest rates

One-Factor versus Two-Factor Models for Interest Rate Hedging

Research question

How much additional interest rate risk can a two-factor hedge remove relative to a one-factor hedge, after controlling for estimation error and gross notional?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Key-rate and factor exposure

For one unit face of a zero at maturity τj\tau_j, Pj=e−τjyjP_j=e^{-\tau_j y_j} and ∂Pj/∂yj=−τjPj\partial P_j/\partial y_j=-\tau_j P_j. A key-rate vector dd therefore has currency units per unit decimal yield. For mm maturities and kk retained PCA factors, Lk∈ℝm×kL_k\in\mathbb R^{m\times k} and D∈ℝm×kD\in\mathbb R^{m\times k} for kk hedge instruments. Use matching yield units and loading normalization when solving the exposure equations.

Feasible hedges

Inspect rank and conditioning of Lk⊤DL_k^\top D, apply declared notional limits, and report infeasibility. Estimate only from data available before the evaluated shock. Reprice original quantities under the next curve at fixed maturities; no elapsed holding-period return. Compare residual P&L, omitted-factor and convexity effects and sensitivity to loading estimation.

Research checks

Do not treat physical PCA loadings as risk-neutral parameters. Unlike D07.01.02, compare statistical factor spanning with zero-bond hedges rather than single-curve swap DV01.
