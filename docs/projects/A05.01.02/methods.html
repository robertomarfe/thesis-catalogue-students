Can One-Factor Interest Rate Models Explain the Dynamics of the Yield Curve?

How much variation across Treasury maturities can one affine factor explain, and which systematic curve movements remain unexplained?

Theory & methods

Open methods.pdfOpen methods.md

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Rank restriction

For mm maturities and nn dates, form the n×mn\times m matrix YY. With maturity-specific intercepts and a scalar factor, centered YY has rank at most one absent measurement noise. Vasicek imposes bj=(1−e−κQτj)/(κQτj)b_j=(1-e^{-\kappa_Q\tau_j})/(\kappa_Q\tau_j) for positive κQ\kappa_Q. Conditional on bb, estimate date scores by projection. An unconstrained intercept makes this a loading-shape diagnostic, not a complete no-arbitrage calibration.

Held-out reconstruction

Estimate the training mean and PCA eigenspace only on training dates; freeze them. Same-date reconstruction using the whole vector is not a temporal forecast. With a single observed two-year yield, estimate its mapping to the remaining maturities using training observations only. Use comparable bp errors, maturity-specific residual covariance and rank diagnostics.

Research checks

Report unrestricted PCA as a rank-matched comparator. Separate same-date reconstruction from held-out maturity prediction; do not claim the fitted loading uniquely identifies physical or risk-neutral dynamics.
