Asset pricing · Consumption-based asset pricing and equity term structures · Level 2/3

Data: Synthetic core; no mandatory external data. Status: current review draft; supervisor approval pending.

Motivation

Asset pricing depends on the consumption exposure of the marginal investor. Changing shareholder consumption covariance with dividends can alter priced risk even when the aggregate dividend distribution stays fixed.

Research question

How does shareholder consumption exposure to aggregate dividend shocks affect the equity premium and risk-free rate in a transparent limited-participation pricing experiment?

Economic mechanism

For jointly Gaussian log shareholder consumption and dividend growth (gs,gd)(g_s,g_d), CRRA gives m=βe−γgsm=\beta e^{-\gamma g_s}.

Research design

IID Gaussian shareholder and aggregate consumption benchmark; fixed dividend marginal, exposure and mean-matched scenarios, beta-rate diagnostic and MC.

Show FMP equality and zero covariance zero premium; report matched expected gross growth, covariance and q_d existence across four cases. Separate variance-only and covariance changes (when feasible), and beta-adjusted safe-rate diagnostic with bounds. Verify pricing moments by independent Gaussian integration or MC and report price-existence failures.

Required exhibits

Equity premium/rf surfaces, admissibility map, FMP comparison, covariance decomposition and MC check.

Minimum viable thesis

IID CRRA analytic benchmark and four shareholder scenarios with price existence and MC checks.

Scope and feasibility

Shareholder consumption is exogenous, not derived from equilibrium labor income or participation decisions. Positive shareholder wealth/consumption not a full aggregation identity. Do not interpret increased total volatility as priced covariance automatically.

Prerequisites: Consumption-based pricing, lognormal moments, Python.

Optional extension: Two household types with explicit resource feasibility in a finite-state economy; solve endowment allocation and prices.

Milestones

W1-3: theory; W4-6: exact pricing; W7-9: scenarios; W10-12: MC and economic checks; W13-16: writing.
