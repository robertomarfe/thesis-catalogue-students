Limited Market Participation and Consumption-Based Pricing

Research question

How does shareholder consumption exposure to aggregate dividend shocks affect the equity premium and risk-free rate in a transparent limited-participation pricing experiment?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

For jointly Gaussian log shareholder consumption and dividend growth (gs,gd)(g_s,g_d), CRRA gives m=βe−γgsm=\beta e^{-\gamma g_s}.

Let q=E[megd]<1q=E[m e^{g_d}]<1 and RD=egd/qR_D=e^{g_d}/q for the iid perpetuity. Then E[RD]/Rf=exp(γCov(gs,gd))E[R_D]/R_f=\exp(\gamma\operatorname{Cov}(g_s,g_d)). Zero covariance gives zero premium. Gross-mean matching uses μs=μc+(σc2−σs2)/2\mu_s=\mu_c+(\sigma_c^2-\sigma_s^2)/2. A rate-matched beta may be infeasible; retain that result. This is an exogenous marginal-investor pricing experiment, not an equilibrium with resource clearing.

Research checks

Show FMP equality and zero covariance zero premium; report matched expected gross growth, covariance and q_d existence across four cases. Separate variance-only and covariance changes (when feasible), and beta-adjusted safe-rate diagnostic with bounds. Verify pricing moments by independent Gaussian integration or MC and report price-existence failures.
