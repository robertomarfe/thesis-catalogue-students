Goal

Factor premia may vary with macro-financial state and their own lagged performance. The question is whether regularized forecasts generalize across distinct factor payoffs, not whether a selected winning factor is predictable.

Research question: Do ridge and LASSO forecasts of monthly SMB, HML and momentum improve over factor-specific historical means and fixed OLS models in a genuinely held-out period?

Setup check

Check that three artificial factor returns share one date and decimal units. This artificial example does not validate the full research design or actual data.

Student-owned research

Check factor signs, dates, percent scaling, source vintage and lags. Keep all three factor loss gains together in resampling. Freeze OLS specification; negative R2 and weak gains allowed; no selected winner p-value.

  1. Construct one-month-ahead factor labels, predictor timing and common sample.

  2. Implement historical mean, OLS with same X, ridge and LASSO; use a prespecified penalty grid and rolling-origin validation.

  3. Report factor-by-factor OOS R2, MAE, cumulative squared loss and paired block intervals.

  4. Report joint evidence across three factors using a common-date max-statistic or multiple-testing adjustment.

  5. Inspect coefficients and stability, and optionally compute risk-scaled factor exposure using lagged risk and declared leverage/costs.

Minimum viable thesis: Three factors, four benchmarks/models, nested chronological validation, untouched holdout and joint uncertainty assessment; negative R2 acceptable.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
