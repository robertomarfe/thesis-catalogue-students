Momentum and the Limits of Fundamental Factor Models

Research question

Does a traded momentum factor improve the pricing of momentum portfolios and independent industry portfolios relative to FF3 and FF5, and how stable is the improvement?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Incremental pricing

In each pair estimate xit=αi+βi⊤ft+ϵitx_{it}=\alpha_i+\beta_i^\top f_t+\epsilon_{it} and xit=αi++βi+⊤ft+ciMomt+ϵit+x_{it}=\alpha_i^++\beta_i^{+\top}f_t+c_iMom_t+\epsilon_{it}^+. Addition of a regressor cannot lower the in-sample unadjusted R2R^2; it need not reduce the joint vector of pricing errors. Compare RMSEα\mathrm{RMSE}_\alpha and paired individual alphas. A GRS rejection tests all alphas jointly under its maintained assumptions.

Factor spanning

Regress the traded momentum spread on the same base factors. The intercept measures the mean of its residual payoff after a linear factor hedge. The spanning restriction is not equivalent to pricing the two panels. Moment estimates and residual covariances share dates, so re-estimate both nested models and the spanning regression on common resampled time blocks. The published Mom series is already a long-short payoff.

Research checks

Use the same base SMB within each nested pair and same RF for long-only test assets. Report two separate panels and paired uncertainty; distinguish momentum-formation sorts from evidence outside those sorts, without calling industries statistically independent.
