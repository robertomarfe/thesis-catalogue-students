D02.02.01 | Student research starter

Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

A flexible parametric smile can interpolate liquid strikes yet behave poorly where quotes are sparse. SVI and SABR encode shape differently and can be compared under the same quote information and noise. The goal is to evaluate the fit–stability trade-off rather than claim that either raw slice parameterization produces a globally arbitrage-free surface.

Research question: Which of raw SVI and lognormal SABR provides more stable and accurate smile estimates when quotes are sparse or noisy? Does the ranking change with maturity, asymmetry and the loss function?

Student-owned research

Freeze skew regimes, sparsity, noise scales and held-out wing strikes. Compare equal computational budgets and report invalid inversions, failed fits and finite-grid arbitrage rather than claiming smoothness implies absence of arbitrage.

  1. Derive SVI and SABR parameter roles; implement stable ATM evaluation and Black inversion, with price round-trip checks.

  2. Fit each slice by multistart, comparing IV RMSE and price loss normalized by forward; report convergence, active bounds and parameter dispersion.

  3. Evaluate held-out strikes against clean truth; distinguish interpolation from wing extrapolation.

  4. On a dense grid calculate monotonicity, convexity and calendar violations in undiscounted forward-normalized call prices; compare fit accuracy conditional on admissibility.

  5. Repeat under quote sparsity/noise and maturities, retaining failed fits as outcomes rather than silently deleting them.

Minimum viable thesis: Two calibrated smile families, verified pricing reference, at least two maturities and skew regimes, sparse/noisy train versus untouched test comparisons, explicit arbitrage and failure diagnostics. No need for a globally arbitrage-free surface or live market data.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
