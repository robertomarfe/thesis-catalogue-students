Jump Risk and the Pricing of Equity Options

How do jump intensity, mean jump size and jump dispersion affect equity option prices and implied-volatility skew at a fixed total instantaneous log-return variance?

Compensated jump dynamics

Under ℚ\mathbb Q, write log jump Y∼N(m,sJ2)Y\sim N(m,s_J^2), a=𝔼[eY]=em+sJ2/2a=\mathbb E[e^Y]=e^{m+s_J^2/2}, and Poisson intensity λ\lambda. The compensated stock dynamics are dSt/St−=[r−q−λ(a−1)]dt+σdWt+(eY−1)dNt.dS_t/S_{t-}=[r-q-\lambda(a-1)]dt+\sigma dW_t+(e^Y-1)dN_t. Conditional on NT=nN_T=n, the log spot is Gaussian with mean logS0+(r−q−λ(a−1)−σ2/2)T+nm\log S_0+(r-q-\lambda(a-1)-\sigma^2/2)T+nm and variance σ2T+nsJ2\sigma^2T+ns_J^2. Derive the mixture price as a Poisson-weighted sum of conditional lognormal call values. The forward is S0e(r−q)TS_0 e^{(r-q)T}. When truncating, bound the omitted stock leg, whose Poisson weights are tilted by ana^n.

Controlled variance

Independence gives annual log variance v=σ2+λ(m2+sJ2)v=\sigma^2+\lambda(m^2+s_J^2); preserve vv when changing the jump distribution, rejecting infeasible negative residual diffusion variance. This differs from matching terminal arithmetic stock-price variance. Compare to a Gaussian benchmark with volatility v\sqrt v, using identical forward moneyness k=log(K/F)k=\log(K/F). Verify the zero-intensity limit, put–call parity and independent exact terminal simulation. Short-maturity wings may be economically informative, but extremely small time values can make implied-volatility inversion unstable. Do not interpret the chosen pricing intensity as a physical crash probability.

Research checks

Build admissible variance-matched configurations before calculating prices. Hold forward fixed and compare several maturities; report ordinary and stock-weighted truncation diagnostics. Keep jump-size and intensity perturbations distinct.
