Orthogonal residual portfolios

Write the vector regression f2=a+Bf1+ef_2=a+Bf_1+e with E[e]=0E[e]=0 and Cov(e,f1)=0\operatorname{Cov}(e,f_1)=0. The traded residual payoff f2−Bf1=a+ef_2-Bf_1=a+e has mean aa and covariance Ω\Omega. In these transformed coordinates the covariance is block diagonal, so

SR1,22=SR12+a⊤Ω−1a,SR^2_{1,2}=SR^2_1+a^\top\Omega^{-1}a,
where SR2=μ⊤Σ−1μSR^2=\mu^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mu for nonsingular covariance and unrestricted portfolios. Derive the identity using the block inverse or orthogonalized span. Zero residual mean implies no gain under these assumptions, not that arbitrary assets are priced. Classical joint intercept inference has the GRS assumptions.

The population identity is not an out-of-sample performance guarantee. Fix training means/covariances and portfolio normalization; evaluate the same weights on later common dates, reporting leverage and realized risk.

Research checks
