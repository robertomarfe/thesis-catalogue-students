A06.02.02 | Student research starter

Current review draft; approval pending. Empirical design conditional on actual access and coverage.

Goal

Style differences in equity valuation may vary substantially by horizon. A matched pair comparison avoids confounding the characteristic contrast with different dates and maturity coverage.

Research question: How do the level, slope and cyclicality of model-implied equity yields differ between high and low characteristic portfolios, especially value and growth?

Check a pair of artificial maturity grids has identical labels. This artificial example does not validate the full research design or actual data.

Student-owned research

Require positive-dividend portfolio IDs and validated exact high/low style crosswalk with common 1/5/10y dates; report both individual curves and paired differences/slope, joint calendar-block intervals and sensitivity across regimes. Keep matched weighting, units and vintage. If exact pair not verified, stop rather than substitute FF labels.

  1. Verify portfolio IDs, maturity units, yield normalization, missing codes and time coverage; reproduce an aggregate curve statistic.

  2. Compute paired mean curves, standard deviations and short/long slopes; plot value-growth differences over time.

  3. Estimate unconditional differences using time-block bootstrap jointly across portfolios and maturities.

  4. Compare slopes in NBER recession versus expansion or predetermined lagged macro states; quantify limited recession count.

  5. Explore whether differences come from cash-flow growth or discounting only if matching decomposition inputs exist; otherwise label interpretation as unresolved.

Minimum viable thesis: One verified value-growth pair, common 1/5/10y grid, paired curve/slope comparison, inference and clear model-implied caveat; conditional on archive. Distinct from aggregate A06.02.01 and theoretical duration A06.03.02.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
