A09.01.01 · Asset Pricing · International, FX & commodities

Global versus Regional Equity Factor Models

Do developed-market factors price regional equity portfolios as effectively as regional factors? Is the incremental explanatory or pricing power of local factors stable across regions and sample periods?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Pricing tests on common assets

Use monthly decimal total returns Ri,tR_{i,t} and a common USD risk-free return Rf,tR_{f,t}; set Ri,te=Ri,t−Rf,tR^e_{i,t}=R_{i,t}-R_{f,t}. The regional and developed factor models are not nested. An intercept is the model’s mean pricing error, not necessarily abnormal profit available to an investor. Compare αi\alpha_i, residual variance, and cross-sectional RMS alpha on the same assets and months. Report factor correlations and the rank of the combined factor panel before estimating incremental local exposures.

Joint inference and overlap

For NN assets, KK factors and TT dates, classical GRS uses the residual covariance and the factor mean/covariance, with degrees of freedom N,T−N−KN,T-N-K under its distributional and covariance assumptions. Check T>N+KT>N+K, rank and conditioning; do not invert nearly singular matrices without disclosure. Resample common monthly blocks for paired comparisons under temporal and cross-asset dependence. Global factors include local constituents and size/BM portfolios overlap factor construction. Alternative test assets mitigate but do not eliminate this issue; alpha differences alone do not identify integration.

Research checks

Audit identical USD units and RF, dates, portfolio definition and factor count. Report N, T, K, covariance conditioning and paired uncertainty. Separate factor-construction overlap from the economic interpretation.
