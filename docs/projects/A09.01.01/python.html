Goal

Investors can hold portfolios across developed equity markets, but global factor exposures need not explain local average returns equally well. Comparing global and local models on identical test assets isolates differences in empirical pricing performance without equating those differences with market segmentation.

Research question: Do developed-market factors price regional equity portfolios as effectively as regional factors? Is the incremental explanatory or pricing power of local factors stable across regions and sample periods?

Student-owned research

Audit identical USD units and RF, dates, portfolio definition and factor count. Report N, T, K, covariance conditioning and paired uncertainty. Separate factor-construction overlap from the economic interpretation.

  1. Align all data, verify currency, sample and risk-free rate; document constituent overlap.

  2. Estimate global and regional FF3 on identical assets/dates. Report average absolute alpha, RMSE alpha, adjusted R2 and individual confidence intervals; test joint alphas by region.

  3. Test incremental regional factors controlling for global factors, monitor multicollinearity and covariance rank. Compare Developed ex-region proxies where available (e.g. developed ex US as sensitivity for North America, noting mismatch).

  4. Prespecify 1990-2007 and 2008-2025 subsamples; report stability and bootstrap paired model differences without interpreting improved R2 as evidence of market segmentation.

Minimum viable thesis: Two or more verified regional portfolio panels plus developed/region FF3; matched USD excess returns, global/local alpha and fit comparisons, joint or dependence-aware tests, and careful interpretation of overlap and integration.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
