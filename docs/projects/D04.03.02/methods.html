No-Trade Bands for Option Hedging

Research question

Can a delta-band policy reduce expected replication cost at a given tail hedging-error budget, compared with fixed-frequency rebalancing?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

State-dependent decisions

When |di|>b|d_i|>b, a reset rule trades to Δi\Delta_i, while a boundary rule trades to Δi−bsgn(di)\Delta_i-b\operatorname{sgn}(d_i). Both use only current information. Opening and compulsory liquidation must be treated consistently, independently of the band rule.

Cost and tail constraint

Use the discounted seller loss, including opening, rebalancing and closing costs. Expected shortfall at confidence α\alpha averages the upper 1−α1-\alpha loss mass, with fractional boundary weight if needed. Select feasible bands by training costs subject to a predeclared tail constraint. Report infeasibility instead of silently relaxing it. Compare paired test differences and risk-cost frontiers.

Research checks

Report achieved risk, not merely the intended budget. The comparison must use the same opportunities, shares, premium and cost convention across policies; this is not a neural-policy training study.
