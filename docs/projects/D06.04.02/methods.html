Quanto Options and Currency Risk in International Derivatives

How do FX volatility and equity-FX correlation affect quanto prices and deltas relative to ordinary foreign options converted at spot?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Currency convention and measure

Let XX be domestic currency per foreign unit and SS foreign equity in foreign currency. Under domestic ℚd\mathbb Q_d, dX/X=(rd−rf)dt+σXdWXdX/X=(r_d-r_f)dt+\sigma_XdW_X. For dS/S=μSdt+σSdWSdS/S=\mu_Sdt+\sigma_SdW_S, Itô’s product rule gives drift of XSXS equal to rd−rf+μS+ρσSσXr_d-r_f+\mu_S+\rho\sigma_S\sigma_X. Equating it to rd−qr_d-q implies μS=rf−q−ρσSσX\mu_S=r_f-q-\rho\sigma_S\sigma_X.

Fixed and random conversion

A quanto pays k(ST−K)+k(S_T-K)^+ in domestic units, with lognormal forward Fq=S0eμSTF_q=S_0e^{\mu_ST} and domestic discount. Derive price and spot delta at fixed k,X0,Kk,X_0,K; check with central bump and pathwise or independently simulated estimates. Random conversion pays XT(ST−K)+X_T(S_T-K)^+ and must price at X0X_0 times the foreign option under ℚf\mathbb Q_f. Zero covariance removes the drift correction, not the discount or conversion distinction. Inverting the quote requires transforming drift, diffusion and measure consistently.

Research checks

Check the discounted domestic stock martingale and currency units. Hold contractual conversion fixed for delta. Validate random conversion by numeraire invariance; do not use physical correlation as risk-neutral calibration.
