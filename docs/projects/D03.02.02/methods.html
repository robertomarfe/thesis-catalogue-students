Volatility Persistence and Long Memory in Financial Returns

Research question

Does a fractional-memory model improve variance forecasts over HAR and conventional persistent volatility models, and can regime shifts mimic its apparent long memory?

Memory and levels

Let Vw=∑t∈wut2V_w=\sum_{t\in w}u_t^2 and zw=log(Vw+ϵ)z_w=\log(V_w+\epsilon), with ϵ\epsilon frozen from training. The fractional integration weights satisfy a0=1a_0=1, aj=aj−1(j−1+d)/ja_j=a_{j-1}(j-1+d)/j and asymptotically aj∝jd−1a_j\propto j^{d-1}. For d=0d=0, the inverse filter is the identity. A stationary AR(1) instead has geometric weights. A variance level shift can inflate a low-frequency spectral estimate of dd without a fractional data-generating process.

Forecast interpretation

Document truncation and burn-in; use the same weekly aggregation and horizon for all competitors. For a log forecast, E[Vw|ℱ]≠exp(E[zw|ℱ])−ϵE[V_w|\mathcal F]\ne\exp(E[z_w|\mathcal F])-\epsilon in general. Use training residual smearing or predictive simulation, with horizon-specific uncertainty. Evaluate variance forecasts using positive levels and common dates; compare the distribution of estimated dd in the controlled experiment, not one lucky path.

Research checks

The break exercise is a diagnostic for observational equivalence, not a test that a particular historical break generated the sample. Retain the prescribed chronological periods and include four-week overlap in inference.
