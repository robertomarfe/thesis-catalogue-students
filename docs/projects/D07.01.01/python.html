Goal

Discount curve interpolation changes forward rates and can therefore change hypothetical swap values even when two methods reproduce all quoted zero-yield nodes. Measure the economic magnitude in basis points and currency units for fixed contracts, keeping the single-curve abstraction explicit.

Research question: How do curve shape and interpolation choices affect hypothetical single-curve par swap rates and PV01/key-rate risk across maturities? When are differences economically material for a fixed notional?

Student-owned research

Verify node reproduction, discount positivity, zero PV at par and bump convergence. Separate interpolation and short-end extrapolation; synthetic fallback requires an explicit approved redesign.

  1. Parse GSW CSV metadata, zero vs par columns, missing dates and units; construct discount factors and forwards on the payment grid.

  2. Compute par rates under both interpolation methods; verify receive-fixed PV=0 at par and annual-coupon cases agree across interpolation at observed nodes.

  3. Compute signed PV change for parallel +1 bp and localized key-rate bumps with fixed contractual coupon; compare actual changes and first-order approximation for larger shocks.

  4. Analyze across curve regimes; report differences in bp and dollars, stress ±100 bp and steepening; relate exposures to coupon annuity and maturity.

Minimum viable thesis: A downloaded verified zero curve, two interpolation schemes, correct par identity, at least three swap maturities and curve regimes, plus DV01 sensitivity and economic interpretation. Fallback: transparent synthetic curves if the dataset cannot be obtained, with amended empirical claim.
