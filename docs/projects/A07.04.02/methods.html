A07.04.02 · Asset Pricing · Volatility & tail risk

Are Equity Factor Premia Compensation for Crash Risk?

Do factor strategies with worse payoffs in market crashes earn higher average excess returns, and is the relation robust to ordinary market beta and sampling uncertainty?

Theory & methods

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

State-price intuition

For a stochastic discount factor mm, E[mrie]=0E[mr_i^e]=0 implies E[rie]=−Cov(m,rie)/E[m]E[r_i^e]=-\operatorname{Cov}(m,r_i^e)/E[m]. Negative payoffs when mm is high can command compensation, but low market returns are only a proxy for those states. Define crash events using a predetermined rolling quantile of market excess returns. Estimate crash conditional mean payoff or market-adjusted residual payoff; an interaction regression additionally describes slope changes. Include a separate crash intercept as a robustness specification.

Held-out evaluation

Construct a portfolio’s exposure from a trailing window and freeze it before evaluation. Compare subsequent mean excess returns of prespecified sorts and cross-sectional regressions with ordinary beta. Report the sign convention (worse payoff versus larger positive risk score), crash counts, influential dates and joint time-block uncertainty with exposure re-estimation.

Research checks

Do not infer compensation from contemporaneous bad payoffs alone. Predetermine thresholds, retain sparse-event limitations and report alternative measures and noncrash placebo states. Factor spreads and long-only portfolio excess returns require different RF treatment.
