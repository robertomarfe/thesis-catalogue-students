# Research plan | A07.04.02

## Study design and independent work

Do not infer compensation from contemporaneous bad payoffs alone. Predetermine thresholds, retain sparse-event limitations and report alternative measures and noncrash placebo states. Factor spreads and long-only portfolio excess returns require different RF treatment.

## Required tasks

1. Define crash threshold and count episodes, show joint sample and tail payoff diagnostics.

2. Estimate unconditional beta and crash interaction/residual exposures using past windows.

3. Compare mean test-period returns of portfolios sorted by estimated crash exposure, plus cross-sectional regressions controlling ordinary beta.

4. Joint date block bootstrap with exposure re-estimation; report sensitivity to few influential crashes and threshold.

5. Compare raw and factor-adjusted exposures, common downturn periods and noncrash placebo states; discuss inability to establish compensation causally.

## Required exhibits

Crash event counts and payoff table; exposure estimates; portfolio return contrasts; joint uncertainty and leave-crisis-out diagnostics.

## Robustness

Avoid post hoc crash definition and same-sample sorting. Tail observations sparse; do not equate negative crash exposure with proof of priced crash risk. Account for overlapping portfolios and model uncertainty.

## Minimum viable thesis

Prespecified factor and portfolio set, rolling crash-exposure measure, held-out return contrasts and joint bootstrap.

## Optional extension

Compare downside beta, coskewness and crash exposure as competing risk measures on common assets.

## Prerequisites

Factor pricing, tail measures, regression, bootstrap, Python.

## Thesis outline

1. Crash risk and SDF;

2. Exposure measures;

3. Data and timing;

4. Cross-sectional tests;

5. Interpretation.

## Milestones

W1-3: theory and sample; W4-6: event/exposure design; W7-9: held-out tests; W10-12: uncertainty; W13-16: write.
