Principal Components of Implied Volatility Index Curves

How much of the variation in a common panel of short-, medium- and longer-horizon implied volatility indices can be explained by level, slope and curvature? Are principal components stable across market regimes?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Curves and units

For index IjI_j in volatility percentage points and horizon TjT_j in years, define vj=(Ij/100)2v_j=(I_j/100)^2 and wj=Tjvjw_j=T_jv_j. A forward-variance proxy between two maturities is (wb−wa)/(Tb−Ta)(w_b-w_a)/(T_b-T_a). Confirm exact methodology and maturity conventions; negative values are diagnostics. Align all series on actual exchange sessions and retain gaps. Baseline input is consecutive daily changes in annualized variance, not levels or VIX futures prices.

Out-of-sample factor compression

Let xtx_t be the vector of variance changes and x‾\bar x the training mean. Diagonalize the training covariance Σ=LΛL⊤\Sigma=L\Lambda L^\top, ordering eigenvalues from largest to smallest. For mm components, the held-out reconstruction is x̂t=x‾+LmLm⊤(xt−x‾).\hat x_t=\bar x+L_mL_m^\top(x_t-\bar x). The test mean and covariance are not used to estimate loadings. Compare one and two factors with fixed level and slope contrasts; three components spanning three maturities mechanically reconstruct everything. Require a fourth series for a meaningful three-factor compression test. Rolling eigenvectors have arbitrary signs; compare aligned loadings and subspace angles, especially for close eigenvalues. Reconstruction is descriptive, not forecasting or causal identification.

Research checks

Verify three distinct horizons, common eligible session changes and the sample before fitting. Freeze training centering and eigenvectors; compare compression against simple contrasts, then investigate rolling stability without interpreting arbitrary sign changes as economic reversals.
