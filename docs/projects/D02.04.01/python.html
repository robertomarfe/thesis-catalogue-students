Goal

The term structure of option-implied volatility reflects common movements in risk as well as changes concentrated at short or long horizons. PCA describes these movements using a small number of statistical components. The key question is whether such compression remains informative outside the estimation period and across regimes, rather than whether a full-dimensional basis can reconstruct the sample.

Research question: How much of the variation in a common panel of short-, medium- and longer-horizon implied volatility indices can be explained by level, slope and curvature? Are principal components stable across market regimes?

Student-owned research

Verify three distinct horizons, common eligible session changes and the sample before fitting. Freeze training centering and eigenvectors; compare compression against simple contrasts, then investigate rolling stability without interpreting arbitrary sign changes as economic reversals.

  1. Document maturity, units and historical methodology for each index; align observations and summarize missingness.

  2. Plot variance curves, crisis episodes and forward variance proxies; flag negative values without automatic repair.

  3. Fit covariance PCA to daily changes; report loadings, eigenvalues, explained variance and one/two/three-component reconstructions.

  4. Measure held-out reconstruction error and compare fixed level/slope contrasts with PCA.

  5. Examine rolling loading stability using sign alignment and subspace angles; distinguish genuine changes from near-equal eigenvalues.

Minimum viable thesis: At least three verified maturity series, reproducible alignment, factor interpretation, held-out one/two-factor comparison and stability analysis. A fourth maturity is required for a nontrivial three-factor compression test.
