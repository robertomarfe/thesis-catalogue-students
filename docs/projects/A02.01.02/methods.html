Long-Horizon Stock Return Predictability and Statistical Inference

How does inference about dividend-price predictability change from one to three and twelve months after accounting for overlap, persistent regressors and joint testing across horizons?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

For a prespecified return convention define yt,h=∑j=1hrt+jey_{t,h}=\sum_{j=1}^{h}r^e_{t+j} and yt,h=ah+bhxt+ut,hy_{t,h}=a_h+b_h x_t+u_{t,h}, for h∈{1,3,12}h\in\{1,3,12\}. Use additive log excess returns throughout the baseline; an alternative compounded gross-return convention must be consistent in data and simulation. Under the null, simulate xt+1=a+ρxt+vt+1x_{t+1}=a+\rho x_t+v_{t+1} and rt+1e=μ+et+1r^e_{t+1}=\mu+e_{t+1} with correlated innovations. With t̂h\widehat t_h computed by the same estimator on each path, the joint statistic is M=maxh|t̂h|M=\max_h|\widehat t_h|. Compare the observed statistic with the simulated joint distribution; marginal critical values do not control joint size. HAC covariance depends on the declared kernel and bandwidth, not merely on the number of overlapping months.

Identification and implementation

Under iid one-month innovations with variance σ2\sigma^2, Cov(yt,h,yt+k,h)=(h−k)σ2\operatorname{Cov}(y_{t,h},y_{t+k,h})=(h-k)\sigma^2 for 0≤k<h0\le k<h under the constant-mean null. The slope score also depends on the persistent predictor. Use HAC with bandwidth at least h−1h-1 as a starting point, not a finite-sample guarantee. Report bh/hb_h/h, Monte Carlo uncertainty and prespecified nonoverlap phases.

Research checks

Check that h=1 target uses only t+1; h=12 uses exactly 12 consecutive months; incomplete paths are excluded. Compare simple and HAC inference on a toy sequence. Verify joint critical value is based on max absolute t from the same path, not independent marginal draws. Preserve negative and insignificant results.
