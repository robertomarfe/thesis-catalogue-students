## Motivation

A flatter security market line challenges the CAPM prediction that higher market exposure earns a proportionally higher expected excess return. Formation-beta portfolios make this pattern visible, while size controls and the published betting-against-beta factor help distinguish different empirical objects.

## Research question

Is the security market line flatter than the CAPM prediction across publicly available beta-sorted portfolios, and is the low-beta evidence robust to size and alternative factor controls?

## Economic mechanism

The benchmark slope is $E[x_M]$ in $E[x_i]=\beta_iE[x_M]$; a low-minus-high return spread is not automatically a beta-neutral strategy.

## Research design

Use the published beta sorts and size/beta portfolios, common French factors and prespecified 1965–1999/2000–2025 windows. Estimate realized exposures and uncertainty; evaluate published US BAB on its own authorized common sample if feasible.

Freeze provider labels, estimate realized beta separately, compare all within-size low/high groups and refit in paired month blocks. BAB is complementary only; do not rename an unscaled spread BAB or infer a funding constraint from the line alone.

## Required exhibits

## Minimum viable thesis

Beta-sorted and size-beta portfolios, CAPM line and alpha comparison, joint uncertainty, FF controls and independent BAB evidence if source access permits.

## Scope and feasibility

Use preformed portfolio definitions, not full-sample beta sorting of stocks. A flat line is descriptive, not evidence that funding constraints cause it. Published BAB is not identical to an unscaled low-high spread; factor controls are not necessarily causal. A12.01.02 studies funding conditions rather than the security market line.

**Prerequisites:** CAPM, multifactor models, regression, portfolio data and Python.

**Optional extension:** Test regional BAB series with common USD factors and distinguish cross-market differences from local sample composition.
