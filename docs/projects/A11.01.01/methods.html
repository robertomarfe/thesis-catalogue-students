The Stochastic Discount Factor and the Geometry of Risk Premia

How do the payoff span and market incompleteness determine the minimum-norm SDF, the attainable Sharpe ratio and the set of admissible positive pricing kernels?

This note fixes the payoff orientation and derives pricing restrictions. No solved experiment is supplied.

1. Pricing as a weighted projection

Let XX be a J×NJ\times N matrix of payoffs, qq the N×1N\times1 price vector, and Π=diag(π)\Pi=\operatorname{diag}(\pi) the diagonal matrix of strictly positive state probabilities. The SDF vector mm solves X⊤Πm=qX^\top\Pi m=q. Introduce the Euclidean coordinates u=Π1/2mu=\Pi^{1/2}m and the matrix A=X⊤Π1/2A=X^\top\Pi^{1/2}. The unrestricted problem is

minu12u⊤usubject toAu=q.\min_u\ \tfrac12 u^\top u\quad\text{subject to}\quad Au=q.

If prices are consistent, u*=A+qu_*=A^+q. Hence m*=Π−1/2A+qm_*=\Pi^{-1/2}A^+q. For full column rank of XX, this reduces to m*=X(X⊤ΠX)−1qm_*=X(X^\top\Pi X)^{-1}q. A consistent redundant payoff does not change the minimum-norm kernel: it leaves the span unchanged. In contrast, an inconsistent price makes the system infeasible. Report the singular values and the rank tolerance rather than treating every numerical inverse as reliable.

For any admissible direction hh with X⊤Πh=0X^\top\Pi h=0, orthogonality implies m*⊤Πh=0m_*^\top\Pi h=0. Thus

∥m*+h∥π2=∥m*∥π2+∥h∥π2.\|m_*+h\|_\pi^2=\|m_*\|_\pi^2+\|h\|_\pi^2.

Nested consistent spans shrink the feasible set, so the unrestricted minimum norm cannot decrease. If the riskless unit payoff is traded, the mean of every pricing kernel is fixed, and the same monotonicity applies to the minimum variance.

2. Sharpe ratios and admissibility

A zero-cost excess payoff xx satisfies 𝔼[mx]=0\mathbb E[mx]=0. Expanding the expectation gives 𝔼[x]=−Cov(m,x)/𝔼[m]\mathbb E[x]=-\operatorname{Cov}(m,x)/\mathbb E[m]. Cauchy–Schwarz yields

|𝔼[x]|SD(x)≤SD(m)𝔼[m].\frac{|\mathbb E[x]|}{\operatorname{SD}(x)}\leq\frac{\operatorname{SD}(m)}{\mathbb E[m]}.

With a constant riskless payoff, the unrestricted minimum-variance kernel attains the maximum Sharpe bound when the traded excess-payoff span supports the requisite direction and unconstrained linear combinations are allowed. Singular covariance matrices require a consistent independent basis; a constrained portfolio frontier need not attain that equality.

Strict positivity is a different restriction. To compute a nonnegative kernel, minimize 12m⊤Πm\tfrac12m^\top\Pi m subject to pricing and mj≥0m_j\geq0. To test whether a strictly positive kernel exists, maximize η\eta subject to pricing, mj≥ηm_j\geq\eta and η≥0\eta\geq0. The traded riskless payoff makes the latter objective bounded. A positive feasible margin certifies strict positivity in the finite example; a nonnegative optimizer may lie on the boundary.
