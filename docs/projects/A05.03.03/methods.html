Macroeconomic Expectations and the Predictability of Bond Returns

Research question

Do real GDP growth and inflation expectations from the Survey of Professional Forecasters improve pseudo-out-of-sample forecasts of future Treasury excess returns beyond forward-rate factors?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Survey vintage and timing

A next-four-quarter growth proxy from mean levels should use a documented same-vintage base and horizon: g=log(X̂t+4|t/X̂t|t)g=\log(\widehat X_{t+4|t}/\widehat X_{t|t}) if those exact forecast horizons exist. This is a growth rate of mean forecasts, not necessarily the mean of individual growth rates. Do not use a later-revised base. Join by actual release before portfolio formation; same-day releases require an established timing order.

Excess return and forecast test

Use rxt+4(n)=nyt(n)−(n−1)yt+4(n−1)−yt(1)rx^{(n)}_{t+4}=ny_t(n)-(n-1)y_{t+4}(n-1)-y_t(1) for quarterly origins and annual holding horizon. Only matured targets enter training; use the same realized targets for the yield-only and augmented regressions. Account for quarterly annual overlap (baseline HAC at least three lags), training-stage factor estimation and revisions of curve data. Survey association is not a structural shock.

Research checks

Require release and horizon metadata before estimation. If necessary use the original minimum of one released horizon and two maturities; do not substitute dispersion for expected growth. Label the result pseudo-OOS unless vintages are truly reconstructible.
