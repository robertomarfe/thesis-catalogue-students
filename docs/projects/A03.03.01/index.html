A03.03.01 · Asset Pricing · Consumption & equity valuation

Epstein-Zin Preferences and the Separation of Risk Aversion from Intertemporal Substitution

How do risk aversion and the elasticity of intertemporal substitution independently affect equilibrium prices, and what changes when consumption growth becomes predictable?

Synthetic core · Level 2/3 · Research draft

Synthetic core. No external dataset required for the core design; optional extensions may require data.

OverviewResearch proposalTheory & methodsLiteratureData & sourcesPython labResearch plan

Research overview

Motivation

Time-additive power utility links relative risk aversion to the willingness to substitute consumption over time. Recursive utility breaks this restriction, yet its pricing implications depend on the distribution of consumption growth and on continuation wealth. The project isolates preference effects in an iid economy before introducing a predictable growth state, rather than treating a richer calibration as evidence of a particular economic mechanism.

Minimum viable thesis

Iid closed form and two-state solution with CRRA check, preference grids and equilibrium verification; leveraged claim optional. Distinct from A03.03.02's long-run risk calibration.

Prerequisites

Intertemporal utility, Euler equations, Markov chains, lognormal moments and numerical root finding.

Methods

Equilibrium & preferences · Factor models & SDF · Stochastic processes · Calibration & estimation · Cash-flow valuation
