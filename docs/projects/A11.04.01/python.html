Goal

Equity prices may look too volatile relative to a constant-discount dividend benchmark. A valid comparison requires consistent real units, a fixed horizon, a terminal convention and a stationary or consistently scaled object. Finite-sample variance differences alone cannot establish excess volatility.

Research question: How robust is the apparent excess variability of real equity prices relative to a constant-discount ex post dividend benchmark to detrending, horizon and terminal-value choices?

Student-owned research

Use identical origin dates for horizon comparisons, report terminal contributions and fixed discount/growth assumptions. Future prices enter only the ex post benchmark. Document stationarity limitations, overlapping horizons and sampling sensitivity; do not confuse this exercise with news decomposition.

  1. Derive projection variance bound and finite-horizon version; verify on simulated constant-discount dividend model.

  2. Construct annual real levels, inspect trend and unit-root sensitivity.

  3. Compute ex post benchmarks for each horizon and terminal convention; show observed and benchmark variation on same dates.

  4. Report variance ratios and block bootstrap/simulation null distributions, recognizing overlapping horizons and terminal dependence.

  5. Test alternative deterministic detrends and subperiods; distinguish constant discount rejection from efficiency rejection.

Minimum viable thesis: Validated projection identity in simulation, annual Shiller analysis with fixed discount rates, at least two terminal conventions and horizon sensitivity. Distinct from Campbell-Shiller news decomposition.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
