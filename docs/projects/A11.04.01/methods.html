Excess Volatility and the Present-Value Model of Equity Prices

How robust is the apparent excess variability of real equity prices relative to a constant-discount ex post dividend benchmark to detrending, horizon and terminal-value choices?

Projection and variance inequality

Assume the constant real discount rate rr, appropriate integrability and the maintained pricing recursion. Iterating it gives the finite-horizon benchmark in the proposal; write Pt*,H=Pt+et,HP_t^{*,H}=P_t+e_{t,H} where Et[et,H]=0E_t[e_{t,H}]=0. Orthogonality yields Var(Pt*,H)=Var(Pt)+Var(et,H).\operatorname{Var}(P_t^{*,H})=\operatorname{Var}(P_t)+\operatorname{Var}(e_{t,H}). This is a population identity for comparable square-integrable random variables, not an exact finite-sample ordering. A deterministic detrending scale must apply to both price and benchmark with appropriate growth adjustment. An arbitrary dividend terminal approximation is not the exact conditional projection. Infinite-horizon claims additionally require transversality.

Convert prices and annual dividend-flow approximations to real units consistently. December annualized dividend data are interpolated trailing-flow proxies; do not sum twelve annualized monthly entries.

Research checks

Use identical origin dates for horizon comparisons, report terminal contributions and fixed discount/growth assumptions. Future prices enter only the ex post benchmark. Document stationarity limitations, overlapping horizons and sampling sensitivity; do not confuse this exercise with news decomposition.
