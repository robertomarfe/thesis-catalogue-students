## Motivation

Equity prices may look too volatile relative to a constant-discount dividend benchmark. A valid comparison requires consistent real units, a fixed horizon, a terminal convention and a stationary or consistently scaled object. Finite-sample variance differences alone cannot establish excess volatility.

## Research question

How robust is the apparent excess variability of real equity prices relative to a constant-discount ex post dividend benchmark to detrending, horizon and terminal-value choices?

## Economic mechanism

Under the maintained constant-discount null, $P_t=E_t[P_t^{*,H}]$ with $P_t^{*,H}=\sum_{j=1}^H D_{t+j}/(1+r)^j+P_{t+H}/(1+r)^H$.

## Research design

Use the specified December annual observations of Shiller prices/dividends and CPI, common origins, prespecified growth scaling and discount scenarios. Compare finite-horizon terminal-price and dividend-based conventions after validating the projection identity in simulation.

Use identical origin dates for horizon comparisons, report terminal contributions and fixed discount/growth assumptions. Future prices enter only the ex post benchmark. Document stationarity limitations, overlapping horizons and sampling sensitivity; do not confuse this exercise with news decomposition.

## Required exhibits

Real/detrended prices and fundamentals, variance-ratio table, terminal contribution plot, simulation-based sampling sensitivity.

## Minimum viable thesis

Validated projection identity in simulation, annual Shiller analysis with fixed discount rates, at least two terminal conventions and horizon sensitivity. Distinct from Campbell-Shiller news decomposition.

## Scope and feasibility

No infinite-horizon claim from finite data. Dividends annualized/interpolated; avoid summing annual totals monthly. Sample detrending can distort bounds, stationarity essential. Future prices used only for ex post benchmark, not forecast.

**Prerequisites:** Asset pricing, conditional expectation, time-series stationarity, simulation.

**Optional extension:** Compare time-varying discount rates or earnings-based payouts with clear assumptions.
