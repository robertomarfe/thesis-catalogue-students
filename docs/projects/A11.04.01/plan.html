Research plan | A11.04.01

Study design and independent work

Use identical origin dates for horizon comparisons, report terminal contributions and fixed discount/growth assumptions. Future prices enter only the ex post benchmark. Document stationarity limitations, overlapping horizons and sampling sensitivity; do not confuse this exercise with news decomposition.

Required tasks

  1. Derive projection variance bound and finite-horizon version; verify on simulated constant-discount dividend model.

  2. Construct annual real levels, inspect trend and unit-root sensitivity.

  3. Compute ex post benchmarks for each horizon and terminal convention; show observed and benchmark variation on same dates.

  4. Report variance ratios and block bootstrap/simulation null distributions, recognizing overlapping horizons and terminal dependence.

  5. Test alternative deterministic detrends and subperiods; distinguish constant discount rejection from efficiency rejection.

Required exhibits

Real/detrended prices and fundamentals, variance-ratio table, terminal contribution plot, simulation-based sampling sensitivity.

Robustness

No infinite-horizon claim from finite data. Dividends annualized/interpolated; avoid summing annual totals monthly. Sample detrending can distort bounds, stationarity essential. Future prices used only for ex post benchmark, not forecast.

Minimum viable thesis

Validated projection identity in simulation, annual Shiller analysis with fixed discount rates, at least two terminal conventions and horizon sensitivity. Distinct from Campbell-Shiller news decomposition.

Optional extension

Compare time-varying discount rates or earnings-based payouts with clear assumptions.

Prerequisites

Asset pricing, conditional expectation, time-series stationarity, simulation.

Thesis outline

  1. Present-value model;

  2. Variance bounds and critiques;

  3. Data and scaling;

  4. Quantitative tests;

  5. Interpretation.

Milestones

W1-3: derive bounds; W4-6: simulation/data; W7-9: benchmark construction; W10-12: sampling and terminal sensitivity; W13-16: write.

At each meeting submit your independent code, parameter/input ledger, results and unresolved issues. Document failures and null findings.
