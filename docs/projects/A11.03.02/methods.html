Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

From conditional to managed moments

For gross bill payoff Rf,tR_{f,t} known at formation, Et[mRf,t]=1E_t[mR_{f,t}]=1; for excess returns xx, Et[mx]=0E_t[mx]=0. With zt=(1,dpt,tst)⊤z_t=(1,dp_t,ts_t)^\top measurable at formation, the law of iterated expectations implies zero unconditional moments for ztz_t times each residual. Finite instruments test selected implications, not all conditional restrictions.

Use the affine candidate m=(1−b⊤(f−μf))/Rf,tm=(1-b^\top(f-\mu_f))/R_{f,t}, with μf\mu_f a fixed training mean. Even with this scaling, conditional bill pricing need not hold: Et[mRf,t−1]=−b⊤(Et[f]−μf)E_t[mR_{f,t}-1]=-b^\top(E_t[f]-\mu_f). Include the bill residual with the excess moments. GMM weights and Jacobian rank depend on instrument count; compare frozen kernels on an identical evaluation moment menu rather than comparing J statistics across different menus.

Research checks

Verify lagged calendar and actual availability of predictors; revised data do not establish a real-time exercise. Standardize with training statistics only. Include bill moments, report negativity and rank, and use paired refits to assess held-out pricing error differences.
