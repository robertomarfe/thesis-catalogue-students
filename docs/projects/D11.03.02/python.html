Goal

A trader who cannot specify a unique future volatility process may instead consider all admissible volatilities inside a band. Robust valuation then responds to the curvature of the payoff, not merely to two constant-volatility prices.

Research question: How wide are robust upper and lower option values under bounded volatility, and why do non-convex payoffs differ from simple calls?

Student-owned research

Use the degenerate band and convex call limits to check sign and units; record instability and boundary effects. Distinguish the worst-case dynamic control from a search over constant volatilities.

  1. Derive HJB and convex endpoints; prove lower<=upper by control-set nesting.

  2. Implement stable solver and verify degenerate band/convex call against BSM.

  3. Refine space/time and boundaries for butterfly; compare with constant-vol grid and report bound gaps.

  4. Plot optimal volatility region and gamma, avoiding unreliable boundary derivatives.

  5. Replay delta hedge on simulated admissible switching-vol paths; distinguish continuous-time superhedge property from discretization residuals and quantify violations.

Minimum viable thesis: Monotone solver, BSM and convex benchmark, butterfly price bounds and convergence/constant-vol comparison. Hedge replay secondary if time permits.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
