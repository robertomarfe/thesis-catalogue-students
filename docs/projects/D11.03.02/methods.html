Robust Option Pricing under Uncertain Volatility

Research question

How wide are robust upper and lower option values under bounded volatility, and why do non-convex payoffs differ from simple calls?

Controlled volatility

Under ideal frictionless continuous trading, deterministic r,qr,q, and admissible volatility σt∈[σL,σH]\sigma_t\in[\sigma_L,\sigma_H], the upper value solves

Vt+(r−q)SVS−rV+maxσ∈[σL,σH]12σ2S2VSS=0,V_t+(r-q)SV_S-rV+\max_{\sigma\in[\sigma_L,\sigma_H]}\tfrac12\sigma^2S^2V_{SS}=0,
with terminal condition V(T,S)=g(S)V(T,S)=g(S). The upper controller chooses σH\sigma_H for positive gamma and σL\sigma_L for negative gamma; lower reverses this. For globally convex calls the upper and lower values reduce to Black–Scholes endpoints. A butterfly has changing curvature, so constant endpoints do not in general attain the controlled extremum.

Monotonicity and economic meaning

Check transition/stencil signs and the CFL condition when explicit. Compare independent solvers and common boundaries; report time and spatial convergence. A single volatility model’s price is admissible inside the model band, but the band is not a statistical confidence interval or a guaranteed empirical bid-ask quote.

Research checks

Use the degenerate band and convex call limits to check sign and units; record instability and boundary effects. Distinguish the worst-case dynamic control from a search over constant volatilities.
