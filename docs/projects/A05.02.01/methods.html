Modeling and Forecasting the Yield Curve with the Dynamic Nelson-Siegel Model

Does dynamic Nelson-Siegel improve monthly nominal zero-yield forecasts at 1, 3 and 12 months relative to a no-change forecast, and at which maturities and horizons does it help?

Theory & methods

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Factor interpretation

As τ→∞\tau\to\infty, the yield tends to LtL_t; as τ→0\tau\to0, it tends to Lt+StL_t+S_t. Thus StS_t is a short-minus-long limiting slope, not the usual long-minus-short spread. Curvature loading vanishes at both limits. Use λ=0.7308\lambda=0.7308 per year when τ\tau is in years, not the monthly coefficient.

Forecast protocol

For factor vector βt=(Lt,St,Ct)⊤\beta_t=(L_t,S_t,C_t)^\top, a training AR/VAR implies a conditional forecast β̂t+h|t\widehat\beta_{t+h|t}. Apply the same loadings to forecast the fixed-maturity yield. The no-change forecast is yt(τ)y_t(\tau). Score paired errors in bp on common target dates, account for overlap, and report fit separately from forecast loss. GSW inputs are already fitted curves; evidence on their shape is not a fit to raw bond quotes.

Research checks

Freeze decay or select it only on training data. A negative OOS result is informative. Do not describe a fitted DNS model as automatically arbitrage-free or an implementable trading rule.
