Nonlinear Latent Factors and Cross-Sectional Pricing Errors

Research question

Can a characteristic-conditioned nonlinear exposure map reduce held-out portfolio pricing errors relative to PCA and a linear conditional factor model at the same latent dimension?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

Write ri,t+1e=b(zi,t)⊤ft+1+ϵi,t+1r^e_{i,t+1}=b(z_{i,t})^\top f_{t+1}+\epsilon_{i,t+1}, with latent dimension KK fixed across models. A linear map has b(z)=Bzb(z)=Bz; a shallow nonlinear map uses a shallow tanh network and a declared ridge penalty. For expected-return prediction use training-only factor mean μ̂f\widehat\mu_f and frozen loadings: m̂i,t=b̂(zi,t)⊤μ̂f\widehat m_{i,t}=\widehat b(z_{i,t})^\top\widehat\mu_f. Pricing errors summarize average realized excess returns minus predicted means on held-out assets/dates. Contemporaneous reconstruction uses realized factors and is a different estimand. Fix normalization and sign/rotation convention using training data.

Identification and implementation

PCA weights on the industry panel must be fit once on training data. Training normalization removes scale ambiguity but not arbitrary rotations without a convention; compare pricing errors on later dates and keep reconstruction RMSE distinct. Do not refit factor means or maps on test outcomes or claim full IPCA/autoencoder replication.

Research checks

Check panel separation (including overlap in underlying portfolio constructions as limitation), training factor means and normalization; perturb held-out returns to test invariance of fitted maps. Use common error metric and dimensions; document identifiability up to rotation.
