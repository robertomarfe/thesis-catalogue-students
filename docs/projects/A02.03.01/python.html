Goal

Forecast combinations diversify parameter and model uncertainty without requiring a large joint regression. Adaptive weights may instead chase noise. The thesis separates combination benefits from extra opportunities to tune after the fact.

Research question: Can simple forecast combinations outperform individual predictive regressions and the historical mean in a genuinely recursive evaluation?

Setup check

Check that artificial component forecast timestamps have a common target maturity. This artificial example does not validate the full research design or actual data.

from datetime import date
origins=[date(2020,1,31)]*3
maturities=[date(2020,2,29)]*3
assert len(set(origins))==len(set(maturities))==1
print('Setup check passed.')

Student-owned research

Toy equal weights sum to one; no immature loss in the adaptive window; perturb future targets and verify earlier weights unchanged; audit common component availability and fixed predictor membership.

  1. Verify predictor transformations, chronological information sets and target return arithmetic.

  2. Reproduce each single-variable and historical mean forecast with expanding coefficients.

  3. Evaluate equal/median and inverse past-loss combinations; compare OOS R^2, MSFE and direction accuracy.

  4. Decompose forecast-error covariance and weights; use paired time-block bootstrap (including re-estimation where feasible) and Clark-West only for appropriate nested comparisons.

  5. Compare rolling versus expanding estimation and crisis versus other periods; optional clipped mean-variance portfolio with costs.

Minimum viable thesis: Seven fixed individual models (or documented common available subset), equal and adaptive combination, historical mean and chronological inference. Distinct from A10.01.01 regularized multivariate regression.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
