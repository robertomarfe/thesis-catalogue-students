Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

For KK fixed component forecasts fk,tf_{k,t}, fteq=K−1∑kfk,tf^{eq}_t=K^{-1}\sum_k f_{k,t} and ftad=∑kwk,tfk,tf^{ad}_t=\sum_k w_{k,t}f_{k,t}, where wk,t≥0w_{k,t}\ge0, ∑kwk,t=1\sum_k w_{k,t}=1. A candidate adaptive rule uses inverse mean squared error on the last at most 120 matured common forecasts, with a declared positive floor and fixed warm-up: wk,t∝(ϵ+L¯k,t)−1w_{k,t}\propto(\epsilon+\overline L_{k,t})^{-1}. Fix the numerical floor and minimum history in advance; use equal weights until that minimum is met. Record each forecast origin, target maturity and realization. A component’s past loss becomes usable only after its target has matured.

Identification and implementation

For unbiased errors with positive definite covariance Σe\Sigma_e, the unconstrained minimum-variance unit-sum weights are proportional to Σe−1𝟏\Sigma_e^{-1}\mathbf{1}. Explain why estimating the covariance can erase theoretical gains. Equal, median and inverse past-MSE rules are the baseline, not the ex post optimum. Fix warm-up and tie/zero-loss rules before test.

Research checks

Toy equal weights sum to one; no immature loss in the adaptive window; perturb future targets and verify earlier weights unchanged; audit common component availability and fixed predictor membership.
