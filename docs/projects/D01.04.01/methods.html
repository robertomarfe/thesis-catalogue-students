Option Pricing with Levy Processes: Beyond Gaussian Returns

Research question

Can variance-gamma and finite-activity jump models with comparable variance reproduce different strike and maturity patterns, and which pricing properties are robust to tail and discretization choices?

Purpose. This supplement connects the proposal’s risk-neutral laws to the pricing routines. It complements rather than replaces the shared F01 methods note. The notation follows the proposal; rates are continuously compounded and maturities are in years.

1. Common forward, different laws

Write F=F0,T=S0e(r−q)TF=F_{0,T}=S_0e^{(r-q)T} and D=e−rTD=e^{-rT}. With LT=XT−Tκ(1)L_T=X_T-T\kappa(1), the normalized terminal price is ST/F=eLTS_T/F=e^{L_T}. A European call has value

C(K,T)=DF𝔼ℚ[(eLT−K/F)+]. C(K,T)=DF\,\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q}[(e^{L_T}-K/F)^+].

The martingale correction changes only the first cumulant of XTX_T. When the derivatives exist, the nnth cumulant of XTX_T equals Tκ(n)(0)T\kappa^{(n)}(0). In particular Var(LT)=Tv\operatorname{Var}(L_T)=Tv, with v=κ″(0)v=\kappa''(0).

For VG, GT∼Gamma(T/ν,ν)G_T\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(T/\nu,\nu) and WW is independent of GG. Conditioning on the clock gives

LT∣GT=g∼𝒩(θg−Tκ(1),σ2g). L_T\mid G_T=g\sim\mathcal N(\theta g-T\kappa(1),\sigma^2g).

The moment at u=1u=1 requires 1−ν(θ+σ2/2)>01-\nu(\theta+\sigma^2/2)>0. The standardized third and fourth cumulants scale as T−1/2T^{-1/2} and T−1T^{-1} respectively (when finite). Thus equal annual variance does not imply equal standardized tail behavior at short maturities.

For Merton, let NTN_T be Poisson with mean λT\lambda T and let independent log jump sizes be Ji∼𝒩(μJ,sJ2)J_i\sim\mathcal N(\mu_J,s_J^2). The unadjusted log process is XT=σMWT+∑i=1NTJiX_T=\sigma_M W_T+\sum_{i=1}^{N_T}J_i, so

κM(u)=12σM2u2+λ(euμJ+u2sJ2/2−1),LT∣NT=n∼𝒩(nμJ−TκM(1),σM2T+nsJ2).\begin{aligned} \kappa_M(u)&=\tfrac12\sigma_M^2u^2+\lambda(e^{u\mu_J+u^2s_J^2/2}-1),\\ L_T\mid N_T=n&\sim\mathcal N(n\mu_J-T\kappa_M(1),\sigma_M^2T+ns_J^2). \end{aligned}

Use σM2=v−λ(μJ2+sJ2)\sigma_M^2=v-\lambda(\mu_J^2+s_J^2), requiring nonnegative residual diffusion variance. For the Gaussian comparison, LT∼𝒩(−Tv/2,Tv)L_T\sim\mathcal N(-Tv/2,Tv). These are three different laws with the same forward and annual log-return variance.

2. Conditional pricing

For LT∣A=a∼𝒩(ma,wa)L_T\mid A=a\sim\mathcal N(m_a,w_a), define the conditional arithmetic forward Fa=Fexp(ma+wa/2)F_a=F\exp(m_a+w_a/2). Let Φ\Phi be the standard normal cumulative distribution function. Then

Ca=D{FaΦ(d1)−KΦ(d2)},d1=log(Fa/K)+wa/2wa,d2=d1−wa.\begin{aligned} C_a&=D\{F_a\Phi(d_1)-K\Phi(d_2)\},\\ d_1&=\frac{\log(F_a/K)+w_a/2}{\sqrt{w_a}},\quad d_2=d_1-\sqrt{w_a}. \end{aligned}

At wa=0w_a=0, take Ca=D(Fa−K)+C_a=D(F_a-K)^+. The unconditional call price is 𝔼[CA]\mathbb E[C_A]. For VG, integrate over the gamma clock (or separate the stock and strike terms using the exponential tilt). For Merton, sum over Poisson counts with a truncation check; a small omitted probability alone does not bound the stock-weighted price tail. Your implementation uses these ideas rather than assuming that a mixture has a single Black–Scholes volatility.

Implement the three pricing laws independently, then design independent references and maturity/convergence comparisons.

4. Acceptance checks

Check the martingale, price bounds and put–call parity C−P=D(F−K)C-P=D(F-K). The call must be nonincreasing and convex in KK; unequal strike spacing requires slope comparisons rather than raw second differences. Check convergence as integration tolerance tightens or as the jump-count cutoff expands. For small ν\nu compare VG with the corresponding Gaussian limit at fixed θ,σ\theta,\sigma; a separate matched-variance experiment may change these parameters. Inversion uncertainty scales approximately as price error divided by vega, so very low-vega quotes must be flagged. Compare independent numerical error with economically meaningful price and smile differences. No full-text review or empirical calibration is claimed.
