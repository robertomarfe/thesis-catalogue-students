A07.01.01 · Asset Pricing · Volatility & tail risk

The Variance Risk Premium and the Predictability of Stock Market Returns

Research question

Does an ex ante 30-day variance risk premium proxy help forecast US equity excess returns over 1 and 3 months relative to a historical mean, and how sensitive is the result to the physical variance forecast and sample coverage?

Bibliographic review (v36, 25 November 2026)

Original references above remain frozen. The normalized records below use publisher or academic metadata, including the preformatted GARCH pilot reviewed against current metadata. Original-issue limitations are explicit. Not a full-text or claim-level review.

  1. Bollerslev, Tim and Tauchen, George and Zhou, Hao (2009). Expected Stock Returns and Variance Risk Premia. Review of Financial Studies 22 4463–4492. Original paper stresses intraday realized variation; this thesis uses daily proxy, not exact replication.
  2. Carr, Peter and Wu, Liuren (2009). Variance Risk Premiums. Review of Financial Studies 22 1311–1341. Paper uses realized minus swap rate; thesis uses positive Q-minus-P convention and ex ante physical forecast. Explicit sign reversal and different object.
  3. Patton, Andrew J. (2011). Volatility Forecast Comparison Using Imperfect Volatility Proxies. Journal of Econometrics 160 246–256. Robust ranking requires a conditionally unbiased proxy under relevant assumptions, not automatic robustness to mismatched underlyings or horizons.
  4. Cboe Global Markets (n.d.). VIX Index Historical Data and Methodology. Cboe . Provider historical closing series and methodology route; see source audit. Actual file rights/schema/history not validated.

Reading focus: Ex ante Q-minus-P variance proxy as return predictor, not ex post variance-swap payoff. Unlike A07.01.02, the outcome is future equity returns.
