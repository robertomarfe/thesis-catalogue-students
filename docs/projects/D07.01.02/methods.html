D07.01.02 · Derivatives · Fixed income & interest rates

Interest Rate Swaps and the Management of Duration Risk

Research question

How much interest rate risk can a stylized payer swap eliminate from a government bond portfolio, and when do key-rate hedges outperform a single DV01 hedge?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Position signs and repricing

Bond value is VB=∑iCFiP(ti)V_B=\sum_i CF_iP(t_i). For payer swap, VS=N[(1−P(T))−K∑iαiP(ti)]V_S=N[(1-P(T))-K\sum_i\alpha_i P(t_i)] in the reset-date single-curve abstraction. Define signed PV change per +1 bp by centered difference for each shock. With sensitivity matrix DD (columns are per-unit hedge instruments), solve dB+Dh=0d_B+Dh=0 for selected target exposures, using constraints and documenting singular or ill-conditioned cases.

Economic comparison

Reprice with original cash flows after the next observed curve shock; this is not a holding-period return, since no time elapses in the repricing convention. Compare residual P&L dispersion, nonlinear error, tail exposure and gross notionals. Test bump sizes and shock basis; avoid par-resetting coupons after the bump.

Research checks

Report initial zero PV for swaps, matched shocks and risk reduction net of exposure sign mistakes. Distinct from D07.04.02: swap DV01/key-rate hedging rather than PCA zero-bond spanning.
