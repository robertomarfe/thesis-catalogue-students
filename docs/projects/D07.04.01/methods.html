HJM and the No-Arbitrage Dynamics of Forward Rates

Research question

How does the HJM drift restriction discipline the evolution of discount bond prices, and how large are discretization errors when a continuous-time no-arbitrage model is implemented on a finite grid?

Drift and numeraires

For σ(t,T)=σ0e−a(T−t)\sigma(t,T)=\sigma_0 e^{-a(T-t)},

α(t,T)=σ02ae−a(T−t)[1−e−a(T−t)].\alpha(t,T)=\frac{\sigma_0^2}{a}e^{-a(T-t)}[1-e^{-a(T-t)}].
The bond price is P(t,T)=exp(−∫tTf(t,v)dv)P(t,T)=\exp(-\int_t^T f(t,v)dv) and Bt=exp(∫0tf(s,s)ds)B_t=\exp(\int_0^t f(s,s)ds). Under ℚ\mathbb Q, P(t,T)/BtP(t,T)/B_t is a martingale (subject to integrability); simulation times must be strictly before bond maturity for the stated checks. With multiple independent Brownian drivers use a vector dot product in the drift restriction.

Numerical decomposition

Construct Brownian increments on the union of the specified grids, aggregating to each grid for paired comparisons (the 12/52/252 frequencies are not mutually nested). Refine time and maturity integration separately. Compare with exact deterministic-coefficient Gaussian integrals. Report MC standard errors and discretization gaps. Zero forward drift generally fails the Q restriction despite the same initial curve and diffusion.

Research checks

Keep the counterexample labeled inadmissible under money-market Q, not an alternative calibrated pricing model. Separate integration bias, sampling uncertainty and runtime.
