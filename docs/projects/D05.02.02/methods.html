Numerical Stability in Finite-Difference Option Pricing

Research question

How do explicit, backward-Euler and Crank-Nicolson methods differ in stability, oscillations and accuracy when the option payoff is nonsmooth?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Time stepping and stability

Use x=log(S/K)x=\log(S/K), time step hh and uniform spatial step Δx\Delta x. The centered discounted generator LL gives the θ\theta-method, with θ=0,1,1/2\theta=0,1,1/2 for explicit, backward Euler and Crank–Nicolson. In pure diffusion, explicit stability requires σ2h/(Δx)2≤1\sigma^2h/(\Delta x)^2\le1; with drift and discount test the actual stencil and amplification restrictions. CN is A-stable but its amplification factor tends to −1-1 on stiff negative modes; backward Euler damps them. Rannacher startup uses implicit half steps before CN.

Payoff and sensitivities

Initialize the call kink and digital jump with specified node or cell-average conventions and asymptotic boundaries. In log coordinates Δ=ux/S\Delta=u_x/S and Γ=(uxx−ux)/S2\Gamma=(u_{xx}-u_x)/S^2. Test errors around strike at positive expiry; report oscillations and negative values instead of clipping. Separate temporal, spatial, boundary and payoff representation errors.

Research checks

Compare analytical prices and Greeks over spot, not just ATM. A-stability does not imply positivity or accuracy. Exclude deliberately unstable runs from successful time-to-tolerance comparisons.
