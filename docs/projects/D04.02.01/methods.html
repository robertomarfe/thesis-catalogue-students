Delta-Vega Hedging under Stochastic Volatility

When does a second traded vanilla option materially improve hedging of a target option in a stochastic-volatility market, net of rebalancing and conditioning risk?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Spanning two innovations

Under Q, dS/S=(r−q)dt+vdWSdS/S=(r-q)dt+\sqrt v\,dW_S and dv=κQ(θQ−v)dt+ξvdWvdv=\kappa_Q(\theta_Q-v)dt+\xi\sqrt v\,dW_v, with correlation ρ\rho. Match stock and variance derivatives through (1HS0Hv)(hShH)=(CSCv).\begin{pmatrix}1&H_S\\0&H_v\end{pmatrix}\binom{h_S}{h_H}=\binom{C_S}{C_v}. For positive variance and vol-of-vol, |ρ|<1|\rho|<1 and Hv≠0H_v\ne0, this spans both shocks locally. Variance and volatility derivatives differ by Cv=2vCvC_{\sqrt v}=2\sqrt v C_v. Condition numbers depend on units: scale exposure columns before comparison.

Valuation and execution

Use Q for prices and Greeks, P for realized paths, and a self-financing cash ledger with costs. Close the longer-dated hedge option at its residual-maturity value when the target expires. Separate pricing, Greek, interpolation and path-discretization errors.

Research checks

Retain small-vega configurations and report unhedged exposures after caps. Compare identical premium, costs and paths; do not treat simulated option liquidity as observed market liquidity.
