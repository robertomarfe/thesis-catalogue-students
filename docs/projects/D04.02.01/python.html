D04.02.01 | Student research starter

Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

Stochastic variance introduces a shock that the stock alone generally cannot span. A second option can reduce this exposure, but a small variance sensitivity can demand extreme positions. The benefit of additional spanning must be measured net of trading and numerical conditioning.

Research question: When does a second traded vanilla option materially improve hedging of a target option in a stochastic-volatility market, net of rebalancing and conditioning risk?

Student-owned research

Retain small-vega configurations and report unhedged exposures after caps. Compare identical premium, costs and paths; do not treat simulated option liquidity as observed market liquidity.

  1. Verify BSM deterministic variance limit, parity and Greek finite differences; cache/interpolate prices with documented error.

  2. Initialize replicating stock/option/cash at target Q price; implement no-external-cash rebalances, close hedge option at target maturity.

  3. Compare delta-only (Heston delta) and delta-vega discounted terminal residuals on identical paths.

  4. Vary xi, rho, v0, hedge strike and rebalance interval; report variance, expected shortfall, turnover and tail ratios.

  5. Test threshold for H_v and capped h_H, with conditioning diagnostics and common-path cost comparisons.

Minimum viable thesis: One target and two hedge strikes, validated prices and sensitivities, delta versus delta-vega on common paths at two frequencies, cost and ill-conditioning sensitivity. Distinct from single-stock discrete hedging studies.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
