Goal

An autocallable note couples coupon and repayment timing to the path of observed equity prices. The coupon cannot be interpreted independently of expected life and downside exposure. The project uses one precise contract to study these trade-offs, rather than treating all notes as the same payoff.

Research question: How do autocall and downside barriers change fair coupon, expected life and tail loss, and how sensitive are valuations to volatility, dividends and issuer credit?

Student-owned research

Validate first-call, no-call, equality and maturity paths by hand. Freeze coupon before physical stress. Report ratio uncertainty and infeasibility; do not confuse a stylized price with an issue quote.

  1. Write a cashflow truth table and test deterministic paths including first call, no call and barrier equality.

  2. Estimate fair price/coupon with MC confidence interval and expected life under Q and P separately.

  3. Vary barrier, autocall threshold, tenor and volatility; plot redemption-time distributions and coupon versus principal loss.

  4. Estimate spot/volatility/rate bumps with common paths, holding original reference level fixed.

  5. Compare with a plain bond plus terminal short put benchmark; isolate optional issuer credit effects and run P stress tests.

Minimum viable thesis: One fully specified note, exact observation simulation, fair coupon and sensitivity grid, Q/P distinction and tested cashflow engine.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
