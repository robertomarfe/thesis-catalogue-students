Neural Networks versus Linear Models in Financial Forecasting

Can a deliberately small neural network improve next-month portfolio return forecasts over linear models under date-blocked validation?

Theory & methods

Open methods.pdfOpen methods.md

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

For portfolio ii and origin tt, let fθ(zi,t)=ai+v⊤ϕ(Wzi,t+b)f_\theta(z_{i,t})=a_i+v^\top\phi(Wz_{i,t}+b) be a small one-hidden-layer MLP; ridge uses the same zi,tz_{i,t}. Minimize squared error over matured training date blocks. On test dates define dt=Nt−1∑i[(yi,t+1−ŷi,tridge)2−(yi,t+1−ŷi,tMLP)2]d_t=N_t^{-1}\sum_i[(y_{i,t+1}-\widehat y^{ridge}_{i,t})^2-(y_{i,t+1}-\widehat y^{MLP}_{i,t})^2]. Resample dtd_t by date blocks, not independent portfolio rows. A per-asset historical mean is an additional benchmark.

Identification and implementation

Include asset intercept aia_i in the MLP as well as linear benchmark. Fix one-hot portfolio labels, lagged own 1/12-month returns, 12-month volatility and lagged market return/volatility. Aggregate loss by date before resampling; seed variation measures optimizer sensitivity, not sampling uncertainty.

Research checks

Assert no date appears on both sides of a fold; same test rows across models; labels mature before fitting; feature scaler learned on training only; check asset-specific mean benchmark and date-level averaging.
