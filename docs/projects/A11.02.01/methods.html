The Hansen-Jagannathan Bound and the Volatility of the Pricing Kernel

Bound from traded payoffs

For any vector ww, E[m]E[w⊤x]=−Cov(m,w⊤x)E[m]E[w^\top x]=-\operatorname{Cov}(m,w^\top x). Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the payoff Sharpe ratio by sd(m)/E[m]\operatorname{sd}(m)/E[m]. Maximizing over unrestricted weights gives the displayed bound. A singular covariance requires checking whether the mean is in its range; a nonzero mean in a zero-variance direction implies no finite bound. Adding assets on an identical population cannot weaken the unrestricted bound. Positive-kernel restrictions are stronger.

Real units and consumption benchmark

Compound monthly nominal gross total and bill returns separately into each complete quarter and divide both by the same inflation factor. Define x=Rireal−Rbrealx=R_i^{real}-R_b^{real}; the ex post real bill is not a known constant real risk-free asset. For quarterly consumption growth gcg_c, m=βgc−γm=\beta g_c^{-\gamma}; the dispersion ratio cancels positive β\beta. Check actual E[mx]E[mx] in addition to the bound: meeting the necessary bound is not sufficient for pricing.

Research checks

Use identical complete quarters for all nested sets. Report raw covariance spectrum, rank policy, bootstrap and labeled shrinkage sensitivity; never claim an observed constant real bill or infer rejection solely from a sample bound gap.
