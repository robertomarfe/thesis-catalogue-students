A11.04.02 · Asset Pricing · Cross-sectional pricing & SDF

Return Predictability and the Joint-Hypothesis Problem

Research question

Can identical predictive return moments arise under a valid time-varying-risk-premium SDF and a behavioral price-distortion interpretation, and what extra restrictions would distinguish them?

A positive pricing candidate

Let Rf>0R_f>0, s>0s>0 and |μ(z)|<s|\mu(z)|<s in both states, with Rf+μ(z)−s>0R_f+\mu(z)-s>0. Conditional on zz, ϵ\epsilon is symmetric with zero mean and unit second moment. A normalized affine candidate is m=(1−a(z)ϵ)/Rfm=(1-a(z)\epsilon)/R_f. Bill pricing follows from symmetry; imposing Et[m(R−Rf)]=0E_t[m(R-R_f)]=0 requires choosing a(z)a(z) to offset μ(z)\mu(z). Derive this coefficient, check positivity, and independently verify the gross stock and bill restrictions for both states. Compare with m0=1/Rfm_0=1/R_f.

The two maintained interpretations use the same return paths. A predictive regression depends only on those paths and observed state, not on which candidate kernel is tested. Additional restrictions on consumption exposure or admissibility may distinguish candidates; a reduced-form distortion is not a solved behavioral equilibrium.
