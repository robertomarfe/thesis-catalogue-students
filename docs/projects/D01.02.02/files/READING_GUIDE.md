# Annotated reading guide | D01.02.02

Bibliographic metadata and stated scope checked against publisher/repository pages on 25 November 2026. No article PDF is bundled; full-text reading and citation/page verification remain the student's task. The following notes describe relevance, not a claim to have audited every page.

**1. Heston, S. L. (1993).** A Closed-Form Solution for Options with Stochastic Volatility with Applications to Bond and Currency Options. *Review of Financial Studies* 6(2), 327–343. [Publisher/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/6.2.327). Mandatory: risk-neutral variance dynamics, transform pricing and correlation's role. Derive the forward-normalized version in the methods note and reconcile conventions. Access through university if needed.

**2. Gatheral, J. (2006).** *The Volatility Surface: A Practitioner's Guide*. Wiley. Print ISBN 9780471792512. [Publisher](https://www.wiley-vch.de/en/areas-interest/finance-economics-law/the-volatility-surface-978-0-471-79251-2), [online edition DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119202073). Mandatory selected reading on implied volatility and stochastic volatility; learn why smile geometry is a price representation rather than a separate primitive. Use the 2006 print edition or documented online edition via library; not assumed free.

**3. Lord, R. and Kahl, C. (2010).** Complex Logarithms in Heston-like Models. *Mathematical Finance* 20(4), 671–694. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9965.2010.00416.x), [author working version](https://www2.math.uni-wuppertal.de/~kahl/publications/complexlogarithmsheston.pdf). Mandatory numerical reading: multivalued logarithms can create discontinuities in characteristic functions; compare a stable algebraic representation with a numerical Riccati solution. Do not assume any arbitrary rewriting of a complex log is safe.

**4. Cui, Y., del Baño Rollin, S., and Germano, G. (2017).** Full and fast calibration of the Heston stochastic volatility model. *European Journal of Operational Research* 263(2), 625–638. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2017.05.018), [UCL accepted manuscript](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1552816/). Read the discussion of calibration geometry and sensitivities before the Jacobian exercise. Their calibration method is not the goal of this thesis; our object is the scaled local sensitivity of low-dimensional features and its dependence on maturities/noise.

**5. Lee, R. W. (2004).** The Moment Formula for Implied Volatility at Extreme Strikes. *Mathematical Finance* 14(3), 469–480. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0960-1627.2004.00200.x), [author manuscript](https://math.uchicago.edu/~rogerlee/moment.pdf). Optional advanced reading: tail behavior depends on moments of the terminal distribution. The core grid is finite and near-ATM; do not present a numerical wing or asymptotic theorem based on it.

Reading output: one page per mandatory source with the precise object, assumptions, equation conventions, relevant finding and limitation. Compare sources' definitions of log-price and forward moneyness. Identify what is established theory versus the new controlled experiment. Literature completeness and student access to full texts are not assumed.
