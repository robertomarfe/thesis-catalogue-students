Annotated reading guide | D01.02.02

Bibliographic metadata and stated scope checked against publisher/repository pages on 25 November 2026. No article PDF is bundled; full-text reading and citation/page verification remain the student’s task. The following notes describe relevance, not a claim to have audited every page.

1. Heston, S. L. (1993). A Closed-Form Solution for Options with Stochastic Volatility with Applications to Bond and Currency Options. Review of Financial Studies 6(2), 327–343. Publisher/DOI. Mandatory: risk-neutral variance dynamics, transform pricing and correlation’s role. Derive the forward-normalized version in the methods note and reconcile conventions. Access through university if needed.

2. Gatheral, J. (2006). The Volatility Surface: A Practitioner’s Guide. Wiley. Print ISBN 9780471792512. Publisher, online edition DOI. Mandatory selected reading on implied volatility and stochastic volatility; learn why smile geometry is a price representation rather than a separate primitive. Use the 2006 print edition or documented online edition via library; not assumed free.

3. Lord, R. and Kahl, C. (2010). Complex Logarithms in Heston-like Models. Mathematical Finance 20(4), 671–694. DOI, author working version. Mandatory numerical reading: multivalued logarithms can create discontinuities in characteristic functions; compare a stable algebraic representation with a numerical Riccati solution. Do not assume any arbitrary rewriting of a complex log is safe.

4. Cui, Y., del Baño Rollin, S., and Germano, G. (2017). Full and fast calibration of the Heston stochastic volatility model. European Journal of Operational Research 263(2), 625–638. DOI, UCL accepted manuscript. Read the discussion of calibration geometry and sensitivities before the Jacobian exercise. Their calibration method is not the goal of this thesis; our object is the scaled local sensitivity of low-dimensional features and its dependence on maturities/noise.

5. Lee, R. W. (2004). The Moment Formula for Implied Volatility at Extreme Strikes. Mathematical Finance 14(3), 469–480. DOI, author manuscript. Optional advanced reading: tail behavior depends on moments of the terminal distribution. The core grid is finite and near-ATM; do not present a numerical wing or asymptotic theorem based on it.

Reading output: one page per mandatory source with the precise object, assumptions, equation conventions, relevant finding and limitation. Compare sources’ definitions of log-price and forward moneyness. Identify what is established theory versus the new controlled experiment. Literature completeness and student access to full texts are not assumed.
