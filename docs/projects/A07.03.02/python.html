Goal

Two assets with the same ordinary correlation may have very different probabilities of jointly extreme losses. A copula comparison separates the marginal distributions from dependence and asks whether Gaussian dependence understates joint downside events at observable thresholds.

Research question: Do US equity industry portfolios and factor strategies display excess lower-tail co-exceedance relative to Gaussian copulas with the same rank dependence?

Student-owned research

Prespecify pair labels and thresholds; do not search over pairs to obtain significant tail dependence. Report sparse counts and uncertainty, and distinguish the conditioning direction in finite samples.

  1. Simulate Gaussian and t copulas with equal rank correlation; validate conditional co-exceedance.

  2. Construct daily aligned returns, report marginals and sample tail counts.

  3. Fit Gaussian/t via pseudo-likelihood on training data; compare held-out empirical and predicted co-exceedances and joint-tail calibration.

  4. Use paired block bootstrap for finite-q intervals and compare upper vs lower tails.

  5. Refit on standardized GARCH residuals or volatility-scaled returns to assess marginal heteroskedasticity contribution.

Minimum viable thesis: Three industry pairs and two market-factor pairs, Gaussian/t copula comparison, empirical co-exceedance and uncertainty at .05/.10. Distinct from coskewness pricing A07.03.01.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
