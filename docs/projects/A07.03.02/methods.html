Tail Dependence in Equity Portfolios and Factor Strategies

Dependence conditional on marginals

For continuous marginals, U=F1(R1)U=F_1(R_1) and V=F2(R2)V=F_2(R_2) have uniform laws. In practice, fix a training empirical CDF convention and avoid 0 and 1 in inverse transforms. For elliptical copulas the population relation between Kendall’s tau and latent correlation is ρ=sin(πτ/2)\rho=\sin(\pi\tau/2). Gaussian tail dependence is zero when |ρ|<1|\rho|<1; the Student-t limit is 2tν+1(−(ν+1)(1−ρ)/(1+ρ))2t_{\nu+1}(-\sqrt{(\nu+1)(1-\rho)/(1+\rho)}) for degrees of freedom ν>0\nu>0.

Held-out comparison

At each positive threshold qq, compare the fitted conditional co-exceedance with joint counts relative to the count of the conditioning event. Report both marginal tail frequencies and the effective denominator. Distinguish finite-threshold prediction from an extrapolated asymptotic parameter. Use joint time blocks, common pairs, and a separate sensitivity to volatility-standardized returns to assess marginal dynamics.

Research checks

Prespecify pair labels and thresholds; do not search over pairs to obtain significant tail dependence. Report sparse counts and uncertainty, and distinguish the conditioning direction in finite samples.
