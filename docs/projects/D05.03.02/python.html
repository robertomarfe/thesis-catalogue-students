Goal

Cosine expansion replaces inversion by a finite series of payoff coefficients and characteristic-function values. Rare jumps can make a visually converged series inaccurate because important probability mass lies outside the approximation interval. The study examines interval width and series length separately.

Research question: When does COS pricing achieve a given error tolerance faster than direct Fourier quadrature, and which truncation choices fail for non-Gaussian returns?

Student-owned research

Inspect short maturity, rare jumps and deep strikes before asserting convergence. A wider interval may require more terms; do not claim universal exponential convergence.

  1. Derive COS coefficients for a put and verify normalization of phi and martingale moment.

  2. Implement vectorized COS and direct quadrature; verify put-call parity.

  3. Establish independent reference prices and error tolerances in absolute price and relative error away from tiny values.

  4. Sweep N holding interval fixed and interval holding N large; plot error maps, identify cancellation and short-expiry failures.

  5. Benchmark one strike and a 100-strike grid including setup cost; report minimum runtime meeting a fixed error tolerance.

Minimum viable thesis: BSM and Merton, independent analytic/mixture references, COS and quadrature with convergence and fair runtime comparison.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
