Does Downside Beta Explain Cross-Sectional Stock Returns?

Research question

Does downside beta help explain cross-sectional mean excess returns of public US equity portfolios after controlling for conventional market beta?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Conditional exposure

Use excess simple returns and a common state indicator Dt=𝟏{rM,te<0}D_t=\mathbf1_{\{r_{M,t}^e<0\}}. Compute the conditional covariance and variance using conditional means. Conventional beta uses all valid dates; downside beta uses the common downside subset. Enforce the required minimum downside count and flag near-zero market variance. Define the relative exposure di=βi−−βid_i=\beta_i^- -\beta_i to separate ordinary market exposure from incremental asymmetry.

Cross-sectional pricing

At month t+1t+1, estimate ri,t+1e=λ0,t+1+λM,t+1β̂i,t+λd,t+1d̂i,t+ϵi,t+1r_{i,t+1}^e=\lambda_{0,t+1}+\lambda_{M,t+1}\widehat\beta_{i,t}+\lambda_{d,t+1}\widehat d_{i,t}+\epsilon_{i,t+1}. Compare the nested beta-only model on the same assets and dates. Average slopes across months, report economic magnitude and pricing errors, and use time-series uncertainty with joint date resampling and exposure re-estimation. Small cross-sectional dispersion can make the added slope weakly identified.

Research checks

Keep daily estimation and monthly evaluation distinct. Verify portfolio definitions and RF units, report conditional counts and collinearity, and do not infer individual-stock pricing or structural risk compensation from portfolio associations.
