Asymmetric Volatility and the Leverage Effect

Do GJR-GARCH and EGARCH improve next-day and multi-day variance forecasts relative to symmetric GARCH, especially after negative market innovations?

News impact and admissibility

Write the demeaned daily log return as ut=htztu_t=\sqrt{h_t}z_t, where ht=ht|t−1h_t=h_{t|t-1} and the standardized shock has mean zero and variance one. For GJR require ω>0\omega>0, α≥0\alpha\ge0, α+γ≥0\alpha+\gamma\ge0 and β≥0\beta\ge0. With symmetric shocks, finite unconditional variance requires α+β+γ/2<1\alpha+\beta+\gamma/2<1. EGARCH instead specifies loght+1=ω+βloght+α(|zt|−E|zt|)+γzt\log h_{t+1}=\omega+\beta\log h_t+\alpha(|z_t|-E|z_t|)+\gamma z_t. Here negative γ\gamma amplifies negative news. Persistence of log variance does not by itself guarantee a finite unconditional variance of returns.

Forecast and loss

Use the same mean convention and innovation distribution across models. Sum expected conditional second moments for a five-day variance target; use conditional simulation for nonlinear EGARCH rather than exponentiating expected log variance. For positive prediction ff and nonnegative outcome yy, use ℓ(y,f)=logf+y/f\ell(y,f)=\log f+y/f and MSE on identical horizons. Condition loss comparisons on the sign known at formation, not the subsequent realization.

Research checks

Plot news-impact curves at a common prior variance. Report optimization failures and standardized residual checks. Sign-specific forecast improvement is predictive evidence, not proof of financial leverage as a causal mechanism. Use paired time blocks or HAC for overlapping outcomes.
