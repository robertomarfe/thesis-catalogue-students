Risk-Neutral Probabilities and Change of Numeraire

Research question

How can one verify that money-market and forward-measure valuation give identical prices even when interest rates are stochastic?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Change of measure

For a strictly positive self-financing traded numeraire NN, the Radon–Nikodym density under the money-market measure is

LT=NT/BTN0/B0.L_T=\frac{N_T/B_T}{N_0/B_0}.
Since N/BN/B is a ℚB\mathbb Q^B martingale, for child jj of node ii the transition is pijN=pijB(Nj/Bj)/(Ni/Bi)p^N_{ij}=p^B_{ij}(N_j/B_j)/(N_i/B_i). Verify positive probabilities summing to one nodewise. In the TT-bond measure NT=1N_T=1, so the payoff is measured in units of the bond and Vt=P(t,T)𝔼tℚT[HT]V_t=P(t,T)\mathbb E_t^{\mathbb Q^T}[H_T].

Replication and consistency

Use the node’s stock and locally riskless money-market investment to solve the two child payoff equations whenever the local payoff matrix is nonsingular. The portfolio cost must agree with each measure’s conditional valuation. A stock numeraire must be positive and tradable including reinvested cash flows; the design has no dividends. Do not discount by the initial constant rate when future short rates differ by node.

Research checks

Check all three measure transformations at every node, local payoff rank, transition sums, price equality and replication residuals. Extend only after the two-period benchmark is independently reproduced.
