Black-Litterman Portfolio Choice under Uncertain Expected Returns

How do prior strength and uncertainty in relative-return views affect Black-Litterman posterior allocations and their held-out economic performance?

Research overview

Motivation

Portfolio choice based on estimated expected returns is vulnerable to sampling error. Black-Litterman uses a reference portfolio and noisy relative-return views to regularize estimates of the mean. Its economic value depends on whether those views improve held-out allocation decisions after constraints and costs. The project distinguishes a genuine sensitivity to prior uncertainty from a parameter that cancels by construction.

Minimum viable thesis

Validated posterior, one relative-view pair, at least three confidence settings and long-only rolling comparison with prior/equal/sample-mean benchmarks; show tau invariance under proportional Omega and a separate prior-strength sensitivity at one prespecified origin with Omega anchored at a fixed tau reference. Keep the return covariance and risk aversion fixed in mean comparisons.

Prerequisites

Multivariate normal distributions, portfolio optimization, matrix algebra and Python.

Methods

Calibration & estimation · Constrained optimization · Regression & inference · Portfolio optimization · Hedging & trading costs
