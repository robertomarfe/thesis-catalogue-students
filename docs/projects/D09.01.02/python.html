Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

A covenant can trigger default before debt matures even if the firm later recovers. Short maturity spreads therefore depend on the path of assets, not only the terminal distribution. Distinguish this effect from changes in recovery timing and contractual loss.

Research question: How do default-barrier level and recovery convention alter the credit spread term structure relative to a maturity-only Merton benchmark?

Student-owned research

Show the analytic hit check, bridge versus endpoint-only monitoring and common-path paired intervals. Compare the maturity-only benchmark under identical terminal inputs. Preserve unsuccessful or high-error grid cases and quantify both default timing and loss effects.

  1. Derive Merton zero debt price and barrier hitting formula; validate no-barrier limit and probability bounds.

  2. Implement discrete and bridge survival and compare across grid resolutions; report MC intervals.

  3. Price debt and compute annual continuously compounded spread -(log(B/F))/T-r; distinguish early from terminal default.

  4. Compare term structures across H, sigma and recovery, holding F and asset dynamics fixed.

  5. Test recovery timing as separate scenario using fine-step first-passage approximation with convergence; interpret economic rather than numerical differences.

Minimum viable thesis: Merton benchmark, one early-default model with explicit recovery, analytic hit check, bridge versus discrete monitoring, spread curves for two barriers and three volatilities. Distinct from maturity-only default thesis.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
