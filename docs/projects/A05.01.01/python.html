Goal

Short-rate models differ in the support and conditional dispersion of their innovations. A square-root diffusion can accommodate state-dependent volatility, whereas a Gaussian diffusion allows negative rates. The economic question is whether the richer distribution improves predictive density on an identical observed proxy, not whether a model can price bonds after an unrelated calibration.

Research question: Do square-root rate innovations improve the fit and predictive distribution of US short-rate proxies relative to Gaussian mean reversion?

Student-owned research

Compare held-out log scores, PIT calibration and parameter uncertainty on identical positive observations. Record optimizer failures and missing months. Distinct from A05.01.03: primary target is the whole conditional density, not conditional variance alone.

  1. Derive both transition densities and check numerical integration and simulated conditional moments.

  2. Estimate physical parameters by exact transition maximum likelihood with multiple starts, report boundary/condition diagnostics and uncertainty.

  3. Compare log likelihood, AIC, one-month conditional quantiles, PIT and predictive log score on holdout.

  4. Simulate stationary or long-run distributions, compare moments, autocorrelation, conditional volatility and negative-rate probability.

  5. Plot illustrative Q bond curves using explicitly hypothetical Q parameters; show why P fit alone cannot validate bond prices.

Minimum viable thesis: Two exact P transition estimators on a verified positive-rate sample, held-out density comparison, simulated rate moments and a clear P/Q distinction with illustrative curves.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
