Monetary Policy and the Joint Response of Nominal and Real Yields

Research question

How do the matched-maturity nominal and real Treasury yield responses to FOMC surprises vary with horizon and communication window, and what is the associated breakeven response?

Conditional data access · Level 2/3 · Research draft

Conditional data access. USMPD: same-window, same-maturity nominal and real yields, plus surprise data.

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

For matched maturity nn, define ΔBEe(n)=ΔyeN(n)−ΔyeR(n)\Delta BE_e(n)=\Delta y^N_e(n)-\Delta y^R_e(n), all in basis points, on the identical event window ee.

For a common design matrix containing an intercept and rates-only surprise, OLS is linear in the response: b̂BE=b̂N−b̂R\widehat b_{BE}=\widehat b_N-\widehat b_R. This holds for every coefficient only if rows and regressors match. Estimate uncertainty directly from the difference response or paired meeting resampling; subtracting standard errors is invalid. Breakeven changes include inflation risk and liquidity compensation. Verify actual same-window nominal/TIPS coverage, units, time zone, event IDs and unmatched counts before fitting. If intraday matching fails, a daily-response design for both yields needs explicit approval, with distinct shock and response windows.

Research checks

Validate one actual nominal/real maturity and exact same-window surprise mapping. Estimate all three responses with identical design rows; verify intercept and slope identity beta_BE=beta_N-beta_R and compute inference on the actual difference (not by subtracting standard errors). Report unmatched counts and TIPS caveats; if overlap fails defer or seek explicit daily redesign.
