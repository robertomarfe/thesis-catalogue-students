The Stability of Anomaly Returns after Publication

How do anomaly returns and factor-adjusted alphas change across original-sample, post-sample pre-publication and post-publication windows in a fixed set of documented predictors?

Conditional data access · Level 2/3 · Research draft

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Event time and identifying variation

Let sis_i denote the last original-sample month and pip_i the verified first post-publication month. Require si<pis_i<p_i and define Iits=1{t>si}I^s_{it}=1\{t>s_i\}, Iitp=1{t≥pi}I^p_{it}=1\{t\ge p_i\}. The middle window has (Is,Ip)=(1,0)(I^s,I^p)=(1,0), the final window (1,1)(1,1). If only a publication year is verified, use January of the following year as specified. Do not impute uncertain dates.

With signal fixed effects, bsb_s is the post-sample change and bpb_p the incremental post-publication change; the total contrast is bs+bpb_s+b_p. Adding calendar fixed effects absorbs common shocks and requires residual timing variation. Inspect design rank and overlap. For factor-adjusted outcomes document how exposures are estimated and propagate their uncertainty. Date blocks must preserve common shocks and labels. Selection and dissemination timing prevent a causal interpretation.

Research checks

Retain the minimum 20 dated predictors and original minimum coverage; distinguish a permitted two-window subset from a three-window design. Log every date source and exclusion; do not equate journal publication with first dissemination or the OSAP migration with verified access.
