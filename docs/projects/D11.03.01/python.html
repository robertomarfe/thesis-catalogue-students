Goal

When traded securities do not span a claim, absence of arbitrage restricts prices without necessarily choosing a unique one. Study how new payoff directions, state coverage and bid-ask uncertainty change that interval, and distinguish closed mathematical endpoints from admissible interior prices.

Research question: How do additional traded securities narrow no-arbitrage bounds for a nonreplicable payoff, and when do bounds collapse to a single price? How sensitive are bounds to incomplete state coverage and quote uncertainty?

Student-owned research

Verify primal/dual matching, strict feasibility separately, and nested intervals on consistent grids. Report statewise residuals and the role of new payoff directions; do not interpret a failed LP as an economic bound.

  1. Verify rank of traded payoff matrix and strict feasibility via maximizing minimum pi; include a two-state complete-market example as unit test.

  2. Compute primal and dual lower/upper bounds for digital and call; show feasible interior price examples and spanning conditions.

  3. Add calls one at a time and verify nested closed intervals, inspect superhedge portfolios and state payoffs.

  4. Repeat with finer state grid and consistent new generating probabilities; add bid-ask bands, test infeasible quotes and interpret widening. Distinguish state-grid dependence from mathematical no-arbitrage.

Minimum viable thesis: Finite-state example with no-arbitrage feasibility, primal/dual matching, monotone interval narrowing as assets are added, and a complete-market limit. No observed option chain needed.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
