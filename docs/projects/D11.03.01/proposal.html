Derivatives · Credit risk and incomplete markets · Level 2/3

Data: Synthetic core; no mandatory external data. Status: current review draft; supervisor approval pending.

Motivation

When traded securities do not span a claim, absence of arbitrage restricts prices without necessarily choosing a unique one. Study how new payoff directions, state coverage and bid-ask uncertainty change that interval, and distinguish closed mathematical endpoints from admissible interior prices.

Research question

How do additional traded securities narrow no-arbitrage bounds for a nonreplicable payoff, and when do bounds collapse to a single price? How sensitive are bounds to incomplete state coverage and quote uncertainty?

Economic mechanism

Let XX be the state-by-asset payoff matrix. Over 𝒬c={q≥0:X⊤q=p}\mathcal Q_c=\{q\ge0:X^\top q=p\}, closed bounds are infq⊤H\inf q^\top H and supq⊤H\sup q^\top H.

Research design

Construct the prescribed finite stock grids and strictly positive generating probabilities; add call payoffs sequentially, compare primal and dual bounds for digital and call claims, then vary state support and bid-ask bands. Verify complete-market collapse and reject infeasible quotes.

Verify primal/dual matching, strict feasibility separately, and nested intervals on consistent grids. Report statewise residuals and the role of new payoff directions; do not interpret a failed LP as an economic bound.

Required exhibits

State payoff and prices table; LP primal/dual reconciliation and feasibility checks; bound-width plot by added instruments; sub/superhedging payoff figure; stress table for quote bands and state support. Quantify the marginal value of traded instruments in reducing pricing ambiguity.

Minimum viable thesis

Finite-state example with no-arbitrage feasibility, primal/dual matching, monotone interval narrowing as assets are added, and a complete-market limit. No observed option chain needed.

Scope and feasibility

Check solver status and duality gaps; no interpretation of infeasible markets as valid price ranges. Recompute with alternative positive state probabilities and extreme states, normalize payoffs and prices. When bid-ask bounds are introduced, derive consistent long/short trading costs or use only the dual quote-band problem with explicit assumptions. No price outside a finite state grid is certified in a continuous market.

Prerequisites: Linear algebra, no-arbitrage theory, linear programming, Python/SciPy. Mathematical proof and numerical construction are both important.

Optional extension: Explore constrained short sales or robust bounds with bid-ask costs and extra states; avoid extrapolating finite-state results to model-free continuous-market claims without proving conditions.

Milestones

W1-3: finite-state asset pricing and LP duality; W4-6: build feasibility and replication tests; W7-10: run instrument-addition experiment; W11-13: state-grid and quote-band robustness; W14-16: write and reproduce all numerical results.
