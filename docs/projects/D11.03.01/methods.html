D11.03.01 · Derivatives · Credit & incomplete markets

No-Arbitrage Bounds for Contingent Claims in Incomplete Markets

Research question

How do additional traded securities narrow no-arbitrage bounds for a nonreplicable payoff, and when do bounds collapse to a single price? How sensitive are bounds to incomplete state coverage and quote uncertainty?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Duality and strict feasibility

With unrestricted frictionless portfolios, first verify some q>0q>0 satisfies X⊤q=pX^\top q=p. Closed lower and upper endpoints over q≥0q\ge0 match respectively the maximum cost p⊤wp^\top w of subreplicating portfolios Xw≤HXw\le H and minimum cost of superreplicating portfolios Xw≥HXw\ge H under finite-state LP feasibility and boundedness. If H=XwH=Xw, both equal p⊤wp^\top w. The closure optimizer may assign zero probability to a possible state: for an unspanned claim an endpoint need not be arbitrage-free when the claim becomes traded.

Additional restrictions

Adding consistent asset prices shrinks the feasible set and cannot widen the closed interval. Bid-ask dual bands pbid≤X⊤q≤paskp^{bid}\le X^\top q\le p^{ask} enlarge the feasible set relative to consistent exact mids; justify their long/short trading interpretation separately. Finite support and numerical tolerances qualify all bounds. Here qq is a state-price vector, not a normalized probability.

Research checks

Verify primal/dual matching, strict feasibility separately, and nested intervals on consistent grids. Report statewise residuals and the role of new payoff directions; do not interpret a failed LP as an economic bound.
