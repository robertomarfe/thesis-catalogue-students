D11.02.01 | Student research starter

Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

A smooth option smile can mask severe instability in its second derivative. Recovering a risk-neutral distribution from limited noisy prices is an inverse problem whose economically useful outputs include tail probabilities, not simply a small quote fitting error.

Research question: How accurately can a nonnegative risk-neutral density and its tail probabilities be recovered from sparse and noisy option prices?

Student-owned research

Do not silently clip negative density estimates and call them calibrated distributions. Track feasibility, normalization, tail sensitivity and uncertainty across the prescribed grids. Distinguish distribution recovery from smile interpolation.

  1. Verify convexity and parity for noiseless calls; quantify violations induced by noise.

  2. Fit each recovery method with fixed tuning using synthetic training scenarios, never true test density for tuning.

  3. Compare call reconstruction, held-out strike errors, integrated CDF error and tail probability below x=0.8.

  4. Report mass, mean, negative density mass, grid and regularization sensitivity.

  5. Construct LP lower/upper bounds for the tail probability given call-price intervals on the assumed grid; document support dependence.

Minimum viable thesis: Two known distributions, three strike grids and noise scenarios, naive versus constrained reconstruction, mass/mean and tail diagnostics. Distinct from D02.03.01, which evaluates interpolation rather than distribution recovery.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
