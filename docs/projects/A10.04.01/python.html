Goal

Nonlinear variance forecasts can capture interactions between recent volatility and financial conditions, but interpretation can be unstable with correlated inputs. The thesis asks jointly about forecast quality and stability of attributions, not causality.

Research question: Do interpretable nonlinear forecasts of market variance improve on linear/HAR benchmarks, and are their feature attributions stable across periods?

Student-owned research

Hand-check daily squared-return aggregation and annualization (same for forecast and target); record zero/clipped values; check availability ledger and positive forecasts. Permute grouped blocks, not rows; distinguish importance variability from loss uncertainty.

  1. Establish chronological feature/label audit and benchmark HAR-style forecast.

  2. Fit GLM, forest and boosting and compare QLIKE/MSE on common dates.

  3. Compute block permutation and grouped importance (volatility features together), repeating with different block sizes; show held-out fold stability.

  4. Plot partial dependence with observed support, compare local effects, and quantify ranking changes.

  5. Repeat excluding VIX or crisis observations and report paired block-bootstrap scores and uncertainty.

Minimum viable thesis: Linear and two tree methods, time-safe forecasts, grouped importance and stability evaluation. Distinct from A10.02.01 (return forecasts) and D03.01.01 (volatility model horse race).

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
