Interpretable Machine Learning for Financial Risk Forecasts

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

Let vt,hv_{t,h} be a prespecified nonnegative realized-variance proxy over the future horizon, and v̂t,h>0\widehat v_{t,h}>0 the forecast, both in the same units. Use the original positive-target QLIKE or its target-only-constant-adjusted ranking loss; disclose the zero-target convention. Declare positive floor for predictions. For a grouped feature set GG, define validation importance IG=L¯perm(G)−L¯originalI_G=\overline L_{perm(G)}-\overline L_{original} using repeated date-block permutations within validation periods. Correlated groups, support violations and changes across periods limit causal interpretation.

Identification and implementation

With positive target vv, QLIKE is v/f−log(v/f)−1v/f-\log(v/f)-1 for forecast f>0f>0. The equivalent ranking loss logf+v/f\log f+v/f is finite at v=0v=0; if used, disclose the target-only constant and zero-target convention. VIX squared divided by 12 in decimal units approximates monthly risk-neutral variance with horizon mismatch; do not equate it to physical realized variance.

Research checks

Hand-check daily squared-return aggregation and annualization (same for forecast and target); record zero/clipped values; check availability ledger and positive forecasts. Permute grouped blocks, not rows; distinguish importance variability from loss uncertainty.
