D09.02.01 | Student research starter

Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

A CDS term structure constrains a collection of expected discounted default payments, rather than revealing physical default frequencies. The inverse problem becomes delicate when quotes are noisy or recovery is uncertain. Examine when a nonnegative hazard bootstrap is feasible and when regularization is necessary.

Research question: Can a parsimonious piecewise-constant intensity curve reproduce synthetic CDS par spreads, and how sensitive are inferred default probabilities to recovery and discretization?

Student-owned research

Report survival curves and repricing residuals in basis points. Compare alternative recovery at fixed quotes, rather than relabeling the physical hazard. Retain infeasible noisy cases and disclose the objective and penalty.

  1. Derive survival, expected recovery and par spread; verify constant-hazard continuous result s=(1-R)lambda.

  2. Generate synthetic spreads with quarterly accrual-on-default and bootstrap; check repricing and hazard recovery.

  3. Plot Q cumulative default and survival; compare bond term structures using recovery of par.

  4. Change recovery and spread noise; report infeasible negative hazard cases and constrained residuals.

  5. Compare exact integration and coarse payment-grid approximations; show recovery-hazard identification sensitivity.

Minimum viable thesis: Synthetic four-bucket bootstrap, constant hazard check, repricing and recovery sensitivity with explicit convention. Distinct from structural default projects D09.01.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
