A11.01.02 · Asset Pricing · Cross-sectional pricing & SDF

From Factor Betas to Stochastic Discount Factors

When do beta-pricing and linear SDF estimators produce identical pricing errors, and how do weighting and traded-factor restrictions change the conclusions on common test assets?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Reparameterization

Let xx be a column of NN excess returns, ff a column of KK factors, μx=E[x]\mu_x=E[x], μf=E[f]\mu_f=E[f], Σf=Var(f)\Sigma_f=\operatorname{Var}(f). Normalize the affine candidate to m=1−b⊤(f−μf)m=1-b^\top(f-\mu_f). Then g(b)=E[mx]=μx−Cov(x,f)bg(b)=E[mx]=\mu_x-\operatorname{Cov}(x,f)b. If Σf\Sigma_f is invertible, the beta representation g(λ)=μx−Bλg(\lambda)=\mu_x-B\lambda follows by λ=Σfb\lambda=\Sigma_fb. The same fixed quadratic weight on gg implies identical feasible errors, although coefficients differ. If the traded excess factors themselves are priced, their restrictions give λ=μf\lambda=\mu_f. Normalizing E[m]=1E[m]=1 for excess moments does not price the bill in levels. Positivity is not implied.

With rank deficiency a one-to-one coefficient map need not exist; diagnose the identified span instead of comparing unstable individual coefficients.

Research checks

Use identical sample conventions for both representations and common WLS weights; verify error equivalence before varying weights. Separate free versus traded lambda, report rank, sign diagnostics and joint date-block refits.
