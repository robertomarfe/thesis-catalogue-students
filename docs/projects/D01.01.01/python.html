D01.01.01 | Student research starter

Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

The Black–Scholes price is an equilibrium restriction in a frictionless diffusion economy, not a general description of the option market. Its Greeks quantify local exposures to model inputs; they need not predict the effect of a large change in those inputs. A second issue is whether two terminal distributions with the same forward and stock-price variance imply the same price. This project separates these two questions: comparative statics within the model and valuation across alternative terminal laws.

Research question: How do volatility, interest rates, dividends and time to maturity change European option prices and Greeks? How large are the pricing differences when the lognormal assumption is relaxed while matching forward and terminal variance?

Student-owned research

Separate each input perturbation from a change in the terminal law. Hold other inputs fixed for Greeks; for the mixture preserve the forward and arithmetic second moment at every maturity. Report units and distinguish small perturbations from scenario repricing.

  1. Derive BSM and implement prices and Greeks with explicit rate/dividend units.

  2. Check put-call parity, limiting cases and centered finite-difference Greeks at multiple step sizes.

  3. Plot price and Greek surfaces across strikes and maturities; identify where vega and gamma concentrate.

  4. Hold forward and terminal second moment fixed and compare mixture versus BSM prices and implied volatility.

  5. Rank economically meaningful scenario price changes in dollars per share and implied-vol points; distinguish local Greeks from nonlinear changes.

Minimum viable thesis: BSM derivation and validated implementation, four input sensitivity experiments, one controlled non-lognormal comparison and an economic interpretation of the observed differences.
