Positive valuation and span

Rows of XX are states and columns are assets; all states are possible. With unrestricted frictionless trading and a strictly positive bond, no arbitrage is equivalent to X⊤q=pX^\top q=p for some strictly positive state-price vector qq. With a unit payoff bond of price B0B_0, ∑jqj=B0\sum_j q_j=B_0 and pricing probabilities are qj/B0q_j/B_0. If rankX=J\operatorname{rank}X=J the market spans all claims and qq is unique when consistent. Redundant assets impose price restrictions even in incomplete markets.

Certificates

For a spanning claim H=XwH=Xw, price is p⊤w=q⊤Hp^\top w=q^\top H. Test numerical rank using scale-aware singular values. Strict feasibility can be assessed by maximizing ϵ\epsilon subject to X⊤q=pX^\top q=p, qj≥ϵq_j\ge\epsilon, q≥0q\ge0. A boundary feasible vector with zero entries alone does not establish no arbitrage. If consistency fails, find and verify a portfolio with nonpositive initial cost, nonnegative state payoffs and a strict gain (including initial proceeds).

Research checks

Construct the required four cases and retain actual portfolio certificates, numerical rank and residuals. Vary collinearity and price perturbations. Keep this theorem-focused project distinct from the claim-specific bounds study.
