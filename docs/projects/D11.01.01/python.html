Goal

The fundamental theorem connects absence of arbitrage to a positive valuation functional, and completeness to its uniqueness. Finite-state economies allow the link between payoff span, redundancy and pricing consistency to be made explicit and checked by portfolios, not only by solver output.

Research question: How does changing the payoff span or asset prices affect existence and uniqueness of state prices, replication and the possibility of arbitrage?

Student-owned research

Construct the required four cases and retain actual portfolio certificates, numerical rank and residuals. Vary collinearity and price perturbations. Keep this theorem-focused project distinct from the claim-specific bounds study.

  1. Solve a complete three-state example by hand and match code.

  2. Compute rank, null spaces and state-price solutions in incomplete and redundant economies; verify sum and positivity.

  3. Price and replicate a spanning claim and test a nonspanning one via projection residual.

  4. Introduce inconsistent prices and construct a zero or negative cost portfolio with nonnegative payoff and a strict gain (including free initial proceeds). Verify feasibility directly.

  5. Vary payoff collinearity and price perturbations; report conditioning and numerical tolerance.

Minimum viable thesis: At least four hand-verifiable economies spanning complete, incomplete, redundant and arbitrage cases, with code certificates and a clear proof sketch of the finite-state theorem.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
