A06.01.01 · Asset Pricing · Consumption & equity valuation

Dividend Growth Expectations and the Valuation of the Stock Market

Research question

How sensitive are aggregate equity valuations to dividend growth and required returns, and which combinations are consistent with historical price-dividend ratios?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

With annual simple growth gg and constant required return k>gk>g, the Gordon ratio is Pt/Dt=(1+g)/(k−g)P_t/D_t=(1+g)/(k-g).

At fixed gg, the rate derivative is −(1+g)/(k−g)2-(1+g)/(k-g)^2; at fixed kk, the growth derivative is (1+k)/(k−g)2(1+k)/(k-g)^2. A given ratio vv identifies the locus k=g+(1+g)/vk=g+(1+g)/v, not two parameters. In a two-stage model discount the explicit annual dividends plus the terminal price computed at the final date using next-year dividend. Report terminal share and ensure long-run convergence. December Shiller observations are monthly-average price and trailing annual dividend proxies, not exact year-end payout accounting.

Research checks

Manually verify December dates and units; report missing years and log-to-simple growth conversion, iso-P/D contours and required-return/growth pairs. Compare one- and two-stage prices with identical growth limit and terminal share; show sensitivity to horizon and a small joint parameter perturbation. Do not use historical growth as investor forecast without a model.
