# Reading guide

Read in this order; derive rather than copy formulas. Publication metadata and stated abstract-level contribution verified 25 November 2026, not a full-text audit.

1. Epstein, L. G., and S. E. Zin (1989), Econometrica 57, 937–969, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913778). Establish the separation of risk aversion and intertemporal substitution and derive the wealth-return Euler kernel. Check the time-additive special case; our finite-state economy is a specialization, not their entire model.
2. Bansal, R., and A. Yaron (2004), Journal of Finance 59, 1481–1509, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00670.x). Focus on persistent expected growth and the role of continuation risk. The paper includes fluctuating uncertainty; our constant-volatility two-state experiment isolates one mechanism and is not a replication.
3. Campbell, J. Y., and R. J. Shiller (1988), Review of Financial Studies 1, 195–228, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/1.3.195), [working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w2100). Read the return/valuation log-linearization. Our local Taylor approximation is of the wealth equation, not their full empirical VAR decomposition.
