A03.03.02 · Asset Pricing · Consumption & equity valuation

Long-Run Consumption Risk and the Equity Premium

Research question

Holding the unconditional distribution of consumption growth fixed, how do persistence of expected growth and recursive preferences jointly change equity premia, valuation volatility and the risk-free rate?

Reading guide

Read in this order; derive rather than copy formulas. Publication metadata and stated abstract-level contribution verified 25 November 2026, not a full-text audit.

  1. Epstein, L. G., and S. E. Zin (1989), Econometrica 57, 937–969, DOI. Establish the separation of risk aversion and intertemporal substitution and derive the wealth-return Euler kernel. Check the time-additive special case; our finite-state economy is a specialization, not their entire model.
  2. Bansal, R., and A. Yaron (2004), Journal of Finance 59, 1481–1509, DOI. Focus on persistent expected growth and the role of continuation risk. The paper includes fluctuating uncertainty; our constant-volatility two-state experiment isolates one mechanism and is not a replication.
  3. Campbell, J. Y., and R. J. Shiller (1988), Review of Financial Studies 1, 195–228, DOI, working paper. Read the return/valuation log-linearization. Our local Taylor approximation is of the wealth equation, not their full empirical VAR decomposition.

Prepare a one-page comparison: primitive risk, state dynamics, preference restrictions, claims priced, evidence and limitations. No claim of matching published results is necessary.
