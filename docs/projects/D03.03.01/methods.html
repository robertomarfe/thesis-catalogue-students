Latent Stochastic Volatility versus GARCH

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Filtering versus prediction

For latent log variance xtx_t, update the predictive law p(xt|ℱt−1)p(x_t|\mathcal F_{t-1}) using the likelihood ut|xt∼N(0,ext)u_t|x_t\sim N(0,e^{x_t}). The one-step predictive density is p(ut|ℱt−1)=∫p(ut|x)p(x|ℱt−1)dxp(u_t|\mathcal F_{t-1})=\int p(u_t|x)p(x|\mathcal F_{t-1})dx. In a bootstrap particle filter, propagate, weight by the likelihood, normalize and resample as needed. Monitor ESS=1/∑iwi2ESS=1/\sum_i w_i^2 for normalized weights. A smoothed state uses future observations and cannot enter a forecast.

Multi-step targets

For HH days, forecast ∑j=1HEt[ext+j]\sum_{j=1}^H E_t[e^{x_{t+j}}], integrating over state and transition uncertainty. Use predictive log score (negative log density) for the next observation and QLIKE/MSE for matched variance outcomes. An approximate log-square likelihood is not the exact likelihood; report zero handling, optimization noise and sensitivity to particle budget.

Research checks

Use the specified simulated filter check, compare effective sample sizes and independent seeds, then freeze the model-selection protocol. Separate uncertainty from latent states, parameters and finite particle budgets.
