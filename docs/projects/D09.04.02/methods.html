Wrong-Way Risk and Credit Valuation Adjustment

Fixed marginal default law

For the contract maturity TT let ZT=WT/TZ_T=W_T/\sqrt T under ℚ\mathbb Q. With UU independent standard normal, Y=ρZT+1−ρ2UY=\rho Z_T+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}U is standard normal and

τ=−λ−1log(1−Φ(Y)).\tau=-\lambda^{-1}\log(1-\Phi(Y)).
Hence survival is e−λte^{-\lambda t} for every ρ\rho. Low YY implies early default. Negative ρ\rho associates early default with high terminal stock shocks for a long equity forward; the actual loss effect is a joint exposure calculation. This is an ex-ante copula law, not an adapted intensity.

Grid and reference

At zero dividends, closeout is Vt=St−Ke−r(T−t)V_t=S_t-Ke^{-r(T-t)} with fair K=S0erTK=S_0e^{rT}. Let E(t)=𝔼ℚ[e−rtVt+]E(t)=\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q}[e^{-rt}V_t^+]. Independence implies the same-grid reference (1−R)∑jE(tj)[e−λtj−1−e−λtj](1-R)\sum_j E(t_j)[e^{-\lambda t_{j-1}}-e^{-\lambda t_j}]. Do not discount E(t)E(t) a second time. Use pre-settlement exposure at maturity and upward-rounded default. Across different contract maturities, a ZTZ_T-anchored copula changes the latent horizon; do not interpret that as one fixed joint law.

Research checks

Check marginal survival at all grid dates, exposure distributions, paired loss differences and grid refinement; distinguish dependence from changes in hazard or recovery. Contrast long and short forwards. A common latent horizon across maturities is a possible redesign, not part of the current frozen scope.
