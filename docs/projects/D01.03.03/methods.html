Option Pricing and Hedging in the Presence of Extreme Market Movements

Research question

How sensitive are hedge losses to rare market gaps, and how much can a second traded vanilla option reduce jump-related tail risk at the same initial capital?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Finite jump exposure

Under Merton pricing dynamics, a jump multiplier JJ changes the value of a call from C(S)C(S) to C(SJ)C(SJ), holding time and parameters fixed. The residual against a stock delta is

C(SJ)−C(S)−CS(S)S(J−1).C(SJ)-C(S)-C_S(S)S(J-1).
For small jumps it is locally related to gamma; large jumps need not be accurately represented by a second-order expansion. A secondary option HH allows local delta-gamma matching through g=CSS/HSSg=C_{SS}/H_{SS} and h=CS−gHSh=C_S-gH_S. A small denominator produces unstable positions. Declare a cap, fallback and threshold before viewing the test sample, and report their use.

Financing and tail risk

For zero dividends, start each short-call hedge with the same true premium. At rebalancing set cash B=V−hS−gHB=V-hS-gH; across the interval it accrues at rr. At expiry value both options at their contractual payoffs. Define discounted loss L=e−rT(CT−VT)L=e^{-rT}(C_T-V_T) and upper-tail expected shortfall at confidence α\alpha by

ESα=minz{z+(1−α)−1𝔼[(L−z)+]}.ES_\alpha=\min_z\{z+(1-\alpha)^{-1}\mathbb E[(L-z)^+]\}.
Use a consistent finite-sample tail convention, especially with ties. Distinguish the pricing measure and physical stress law, hold the risk-neutral model fixed in the physical stress, and estimate paired uncertainty for the difference in ES. Gamma matching does not guarantee lower loss for every jump.

Research checks

Predeclare gamma caps and fallback before comparing stress scenarios. Use matched paths, initial capital and funding; report ES uncertainty and the frequency of constrained positions, including the no-jump subset.
