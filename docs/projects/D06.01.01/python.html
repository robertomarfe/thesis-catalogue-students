D06.01.01 | Student research starter

Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

Averaging reduces exposure to any single observation, but moving the averaging dates also changes expected levels when carry is nonzero. This project separates monitoring frequency from averaging window and asks how both price and hedge sensitivities differ from terminal exposure.

Research question: How do the averaging window, monitoring frequency and volatility affect arithmetic and geometric Asian call prices and their hedge sensitivities relative to a European option?

Student-owned research

Change frequency and window separately. Use union-grid draws where practical because grids are not nested. Preserve payment time; do not call a finite fixing grid a continuous average.

  1. Derive geometric Asian closed form from joint lognormal moments; verify single fixing reduces to a European claim when fixing at T.

  2. Price arithmetic and geometric Asians; validate ordering, Monte Carlo confidence intervals and variance reduction.

  3. Separate monitoring frequency from averaging window, comparing each while holding the other constant.

  4. Estimate delta and vega with common-random-number central differences and step-size checks; keep all fixings future at valuation.

  5. Map price and Greek differences from European benchmark across strikes and volatilities; explain results through variance and expected average levels.

Minimum viable thesis: Validated geometric and arithmetic pricing, two windows and at least three fixing frequencies, delta and vega analysis, interpretation of averaging versus terminal exposure.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
