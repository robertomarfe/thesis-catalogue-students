Backtest Overfitting and the Illusion of Predictability

How does the number of candidate predictive models affect apparent OOS performance under a no-predictability null, and how much does nested temporal validation reduce the gap between reported and independent test performance?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

For candidate set ℳK\mathcal M_K, a development winner is j*=argminj∈ℳKL¯j,devj^*=\arg\min_{j\in\mathcal M_K}\overline L_{j,dev}. Evaluate that fixed rule on independent final data. The invalid oracle minj∈ℳKL¯j,test\min_{j\in\mathcal M_K}\overline L_{j,test} reuses the final test and is deliberately not deployable. Under a no-conditional-mean-predictability null yt+1=0.005+σtϵt+1y_{t+1}=0.005+\sigma_t\epsilon_{t+1} with Etϵt+1=0E_t\epsilon_{t+1}=0, compare constant and time-varying σt\sigma_t. Use nested candidate sets and paired paths across KK to quantify search-size effects and Monte Carlo uncertainty.

Identification and implementation

Use yt+1=0.005+σtϵt+1y_{t+1}=0.005+\sigma_t\epsilon_{t+1}, with constant σt=0.04\sigma_t=0.04 in the iid null and prespecified conditional-variance dynamics in the second. Compare the development-selected rule on final data against the invalid test oracle; purge immature labels at the boundary. Report paired gap and Monte Carlo standard error, not an independent-samples standard error.

Research checks

Perturb immature/final targets and ensure development winner is unchanged. Check nested candidate membership, paired common draws, strict boundary purge and same benchmark dates. Oracle never used in deployable policy. Real illustration remains required under original scope.
