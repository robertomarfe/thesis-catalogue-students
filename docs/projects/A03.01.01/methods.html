A03.01.01 · Asset Pricing · Consumption & equity valuation

The Equity Premium Puzzle: Theory and Quantitative Evidence

Research question

Can a CRRA stochastic discount factor with plausible risk aversion and discounting simultaneously explain the US market excess return and the average risk-free rate? How sensitive is the answer to consumption measurement and time aggregation?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

With gross consumption growth Gt+1G_{t+1}, power utility implies mt+1=βGt+1−γm_{t+1}=\beta G_{t+1}^{-\gamma} and E[mRi]=1E[m R_i]=1.

For each γ\gamma, the bill Euler equation gives βb=1/E[G−γRb]\beta_b=1/E[G^{-\gamma}R_b]. Evaluate the market Euler residual at that value, rather than refitting beta for each asset. For Re=Rm−RbR^e=R_m-R_b, the exact restriction is E[Re]=−Cov(m,Re)/E[m]E[R^e]=-\operatorname{Cov}(m,R^e)/E[m]. This is a mean excess payoff identity even if the bill is risky in real terms. Use compounded nominal gross legs and a common inflation divisor; a quarterly ex post real bill is not a known real safe payoff. Convert annual beta by fourth root and distinguish arithmetic annualization from compound returns.

Research checks

On a common quarterly calendar report both Euler residuals and beta pricing the bill, then the market residual and covariance identity; resample contiguous joint date blocks. Check compounded nominal gross legs divided by the same inflation ratio and compare beta annualized by beta_q**4. Distinguish arithmetic premium from compound annual growth. Report parameter grid including infeasible beta.
