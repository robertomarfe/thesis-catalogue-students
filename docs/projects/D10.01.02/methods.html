1. Integrated yield and Gaussian law

Fix the observation time tt and delivery time T=t+uT=t+u. Suppress time tt in conditional quantities. The OU solution is

δt+s=a+(δt−a)e−κs+σδ∫0se−κ(s−v)dWt+vδ.\delta_{t+s}=a+(\delta_t-a)e^{-\kappa s}+\sigma_\delta\int_0^s e^{-\kappa(s-v)}dW^\delta_{t+v}.

With B(s)=(1−e−κs)/κB(s)=(1-e^{-\kappa s})/\kappa, stochastic Fubini gives

∫tTδsds=au+(δt−a)B(u)+σδ∫0uB(u−v)dWt+vδ.\int_t^T\delta_sds=au+(\delta_t-a)B(u)+\sigma_\delta\int_0^u B(u-v)dW^\delta_{t+v}.

The terminal log spot is conditionally normal with mean m=logSt+(r−a−σS2/2)u−(δt−a)B(u)m=\log S_t+(r-a-\sigma_S^2/2)u-(\delta_t-a)B(u) and variance

v=σS2u+σδ2J(u)−2ρσSσδI(u),v=\sigma_S^2u+\sigma_\delta^2J(u)-2\rho\sigma_S\sigma_\delta I(u),

where the integrals have closed forms

I(u)=∫0uB(s)ds=u−B(u)κ,I(u)=\int_0^u B(s)ds=\frac{u-B(u)}{\kappa}, J(u)=∫0uB(s)2ds=u−2B(u)+B2κ(u)κ2,J(u)=\int_0^u B(s)^2ds=\frac{u-2B(u)+B_{2\kappa}(u)}{\kappa^2},

and B2κ(u)=(1−e−2κu)/(2κ)B_{2\kappa}(u)=(1-e^{-2\kappa u})/(2\kappa). These differences may lose numerical precision when κu\kappa u is very small; quadrature or stable series can be used for checks. Under constant rates the forward equals the conditional mean spot,

Ft,T=exp(m+v/2).F_{t,T}=\exp(m+v/2).

For deterministic constant yield (σδ=0\sigma_\delta=0, δt=a\delta_t=a), this reduces to Ste(r−δt)uS_te^{(r-\delta_t)u}. If the initial yield differs from its long-run mean, a deterministic but time-varying carry remains. This distinction is essential for the constant-carry benchmark.

2. Independent price verification

Numerical quadrature of II and JJ provides a reference. Independently evaluate the closed forms above, then generate logST=m+vZ\log S_T=m+\sqrt v Z with Z∼N(0,1)Z\sim N(0,1). For antithetic simulation, average the pair emcosh(vZ)e^m\cosh(\sqrt v Z) and compute the standard error across independent pair means (not across correlated individual draws). This checks marginal prices, not the joint covariance of the forward curve. A separate OU path simulation with correlated innovations is needed to validate the joint dynamics.

3. Sensitivities and curve covariance

At fixed delivery,

∂logStlogFt,T=1,∂δtlogFt,T=−B(u).\partial_{\log S_t}\log F_{t,T}=1,\qquad \partial_{\delta_t}\log F_{t,T}=-B(u).

The diffusion of the log forward for a fixed delivery is σSdWtS−B(u)σδdWtδ\sigma_SdW_t^S-B(u)\sigma_\delta dW_t^\delta. Therefore the instantaneous covariance rate for deliveries Ti,TjT_i,T_j is

cij=σS2+BiBjσδ2−ρσSσδ(Bi+Bj).c_{ij}=\sigma_S^2+B_i B_j\sigma_\delta^2-\rho\sigma_S\sigma_\delta(B_i+B_j).

This covariance matrix is positive semidefinite and has rank at most two. It is an instantaneous fixed-delivery result, not the covariance of monthly rolled-futures returns. To compare a one-factor constant-carry curve, choose its carry c=log(Ft,T1/St)/(T1−t)c=\log(F_{t,T_1}/S_t)/(T_1-t) and compute StecuS_te^{cu} for every delivery. The match at the first delivery is mechanical; differences elsewhere reflect curve shape.
