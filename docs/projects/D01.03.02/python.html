D01.03.02 | Student research starter

Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

Negative equity option skew can arise from diffusive leverage or from discontinuous returns. A fit to a restricted set of quotes may not distinguish these mechanisms. This project compares two competing risk-neutral models against known synthetic truth, using held-out maturity and strike information to assess their discriminatory power.

Research question: Can Heston and Merton models fit the same noisy synthetic vanilla quotes, and which maturity and strike diagnostics discriminate between the two mechanisms?

Student-owned research

Generate a separate Heston and Merton truth panel. Use the same objective, bounds policy, numerical precision and untouched test grid across fitted models; report model-dependent failures and do not select short maturities after inspecting errors.

  1. Verify pricing and IV inversion against BSM limits.

  2. Fit both models to both truth panels with common weights.

  3. Compare training and held-out price/IV errors and short-end skew.

  4. Profile parameters and record near-equivalent fits; report Jacobian singular values.

  5. Repeat noise and strike-grid scenarios; compare short-maturity OTM premiums and explain limits of inference.

Minimum viable thesis: Two synthetic panels, two calibrated models, held-out strikes/maturities and multistart diagnostic.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
