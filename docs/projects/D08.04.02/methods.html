D08.04.02 · Derivatives · Hedging & volatility derivatives

Designing Cost-Constrained Tail Risk Hedges

Research question

For a fixed annual initial premium budget, how do a protective put, a put spread and a collar differ in expected shortfall, expected return and extreme-loss protection? Which rankings survive changes in the tail distribution and the volatility risk premium?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Feasibility and payoff

Use one stock plus financed net premium; an unspent budget is not a cost. Protective put overlay is a(Kp−ST)+a(K_p-S_T)^+; a put spread subtracts a(Kl−ST)+a(K_l-S_T)^+, Kl<KpK_l<K_p; a covered collar subtracts a call with quantity no greater than stock held. The net premium may be negative; record it rather than forcing the budget to bind.

Loss and evaluation

Price legs under a coherent Q law and evaluate L=1−WT/S0L=1-W_T/S_0 under P. ES at 95 percent is the average upper five-percent loss mass, with correct boundary weights. Higher Q volatility is an insurance-cost scenario, not by itself an identified variance premium. Specify the P jump drift consistently with the declared mean.

Research checks

Separate single-horizon synthetic choice from historical rolling strategies. Verify equal net wealth, budget and extreme-spot payoff limits; report cost, expected return, tail protection, upside and paired uncertainty.
