Does the VIX Improve Realized Volatility Forecasts?

Research question

Does lagged VIX improve one-month physical variance forecasts relative to historical and GARCH-based forecasts in genuine chronological evaluation?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Risk-neutral and physical objects

The VIX is an annualized volatility index on SPX option-implied variation. For HcH_c calendar days, (It/100)2Hc/365(I_t/100)^2 H_c/365 is a horizon-variance approximation; it assumes scaling from the 30-day maturity. Actual squared daily log returns on the broad FF market proxy are neither exact SPX returns nor intraday quadratic variation. Record this distinction explicitly.

Forecast evaluation

Use lagged returns and scaled implied variance in a model for Yt,H=∑j∈𝒮(t,H)uj2Y_{t,H}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal S(t,H)}u_j^2, where 𝒮\mathcal S is the set of audited trading sessions in the target window. Fit on matured labels only, preserve positivity through a log link or training-only floor, and compare QLIKE logf+Y/f\log f+Y/f and MSE at common origins. Estimate uncertainty from paired loss differences, not independent errors from separate samples.

Research checks

The value added by VIX is a forecasting question, not evidence that the risk-neutral and physical measures are equal. Treat month-length scaling and broad-market versus SPX mismatch as limitations and preserve missing close dates.
