Financial Stress and the Stock-Bond Correlation

Does the stock-bond return correlation vary with prior financial stress, and does that variation change the diversification benefit of a fixed stock-bond portfolio?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

For simple holding returns and fixed stock weight ww, portfolio variance is w2σS2+(1−w)2σB2+2w(1−w)cSBw^2\sigma_S^2+(1-w)^2\sigma_B^2+2w(1-w)c_{SB}, with conditional covariance cSBc_{SB}.

Estimate covariance and correlation on the same regime dates. Relative to within-regime independence, the last term is the signed covariance contribution, not the entire diversification benefit. The monthly net zero return is exp[pt+1(n−1/12)−pt(n)]−1\exp[p_{t+1}(n-1/12)-p_t(n)]-1. Construct stress from the latest eligible released weekly observation from the preceding month or earlier; observation and publication dates differ. Use fixed threshold zero or a quantile fitted only in training. Report counts, marginal risks, covariance and regime contributions with common-date blocks. Distinguish conditional description from implementable forecasts; revised stress histories imply pseudo-real-time.

Research checks

Use a release ledger or explicitly pseudo-real-time extra lag; set threshold from fixed rule or past data. Compute exact aging bond return and nominal equity total return; report regime counts, marginal variances, covariance and 2w_sw_b*cov contribution with date-block inference and fixed-weight risk decomposition.
