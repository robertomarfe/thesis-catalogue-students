Goal

Financial stress can change the joint distribution of stock and bond returns. A correlation shift alone does not establish whether a fixed allocation becomes riskier, because marginal variances also move.

Research question: Does the stock-bond return correlation vary with prior financial stress, and does that variation change the diversification benefit of a fixed stock-bond portfolio?

Setup check

Student-owned research

Use a release ledger or explicitly pseudo-real-time extra lag; set threshold from fixed rule or past data. Compute exact aging bond return and nominal equity total return; report regime counts, marginal variances, covariance and 2w_sw_b*cov contribution with date-block inference and fixed-weight risk decomposition.

  1. Build discount factors and verify zero-bond holding-return formula against parallel-shock duration approximation.

  2. Plot rolling 36/60-month covariance/correlation and volatilities.

  3. Split by lagged stress above zero and training-sample quantiles; estimate conditional covariance and correlation with paired block bootstrap.

  4. Compare 60/40 portfolio risk to an independence counterfactual across regimes.

  5. Test robustness by bond maturity, alternative NFCI, lag and pre/post-2008 or inflationary periods; report event counts.

Minimum viable thesis: Monthly market and 5y synthetic zero returns, lagged stress, conditional risk and paired block inference. Distinct from macro inflation hedge A04.02.02.
