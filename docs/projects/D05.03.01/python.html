D05.03.01 | Student research starter

Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

Transform methods are useful when the terminal law has a characteristic function but no simple option formula. A large batch of strikes may justify FFT setup, while a few queries may favor quadrature. The thesis compares speed at controlled pricing error, not merely the elapsed time of two implementations.

Research question: At a specified error tolerance, when is FFT inversion more efficient than direct quadrature for European option pricing?

Student-owned research

Separate characteristic-function, cutoff, aliasing and interpolation error. Freeze tolerance and hardware protocol. Use absolute currency errors near zero; calibration is outside scope.

  1. Derive inversion, validate characteristic function normalization and martingale moments.

  2. Test BSM analytic errors; implement two numerically independent Heston representations.

  3. Sweep alpha, truncation and grid size, separately assess off-grid interpolation.

  4. Report maximum and median absolute price error and timings at fixed tolerance (e.g. 0.001 per 100 spot).

  5. Test short maturities, far strikes and high vol-of-vol; flag moment explosions and branch issues rather than suppressing failures.

Minimum viable thesis: BSM exact validation, one Heston reference, FFT and direct quadrature comparisons across strike counts at matched tolerance. Distinct from D05.01 and D05.02 numerical methods.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
