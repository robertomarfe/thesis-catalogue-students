Goal

A larger factor model can explain more return variation without improving the pricing of expected returns. A further complication is that published FF3 and FF5 need not share identical size factors. A literal comparison and a deliberately nested comparison answer different questions.

Research question: Does the Fama-French five-factor model improve pricing of US industry and characteristic portfolios relative to the three-factor model, and are the gains stable across samples? Do the additional factors have explanatory power conditional on common market, size and value factors?

Student-owned research

Require the monthly return blocks, matched RF and common complete calendar, positive GRS degrees of freedom, and at least the original provisional subperiod coverage. Refit paired alpha differences in joint time blocks. Document covariance singularity rather than silently regularizing the classical GRS statistic.

  1. Document factor definitions and common-sample descriptive statistics; estimate FF3 and FF5 for the same excess-return matrix.

  2. Compute annualized mean absolute alpha, alpha RMSE, adjusted R-squared, GRS statistic/p-value separately by model and test-asset set; display individual alpha estimates with HAC intervals.

  3. Estimate a deliberately nested pair with Mkt-RF, SMB and HML from FF5 held fixed, then add RMW and CMA; compare joint restrictions and factor spanning regressions.

  4. Repeat by prespecified subperiod and alternative portfolio family; use block-bootstrap paired alpha-RMSE differences for uncertainty, not raw GRS p-value rankings alone.

Minimum viable thesis: Reproduce FF3 and FF5 for industries plus one secondary test-asset set, compare pricing errors and joint tests on a common sample, include the nested-model check and time split. A five-factor rejection or weak improvement is a legitimate conclusion.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
