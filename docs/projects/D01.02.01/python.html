Goal

A stochastic-volatility model adds economically meaningful degrees of freedom, but a low pricing residual does not establish that the corresponding parameters are precisely identified. Strike coverage informs asymmetry and curvature; maturity coverage informs the variance dynamics. The project studies whether additional information improves held-out valuation even when the parameter estimates themselves remain imprecise.

Research question: When does stochastic volatility improve pricing beyond constant volatility, and which dimensions of a cross-section of European options identify its parameters? Assess the hypothesis that strike and maturity coverage jointly improve held-out pricing, even when parameter recovery remains weak.

Student-owned research

Begin with a checked pricer and a two-parameter fit, then release all five parameters. Freeze an entire maturity and alternating strikes before generating noise. Report objective profiles, failed starts, parameter recovery and held-out prices as separate exhibits.

  1. Implement BSM prices/Greeks and Heston characteristic-function prices; check parity, limits as xi tends to zero with v0=theta, and numerical integration tolerance.

  2. Calibrate a single BSM volatility and Heston by weighted least squares on training quotes (vega-scaled price errors or IV errors, fixed rule). Use positive parameter transforms, bounded rho and multiple starting points; record convergence.

  3. Compare train and held-out price RMSE, IV RMSE (on invertible quotes), and maximum error. Repeat over noise seeds and over a separate flat-volatility generating model.

  4. Vary rho, xi and kappa one at a time; inspect objective profiles and near-optimal parameter sets rather than equating good fit with parameter identification.

Minimum viable thesis: A correct BSM and Heston pricer, one full noise-free and one noisy calibration experiment, a prespecified holdout, and a defensible explanation of parameter recovery versus predictive pricing. Pass numerical tests before interpreting calibration. A null improvement is informative under the flat-volatility generating model.
