Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Horizon-consistent variance

For index levels I30,I90I_{30},I_{90} expressed in volatility percentage points, let vj=(Ij/100)2v_j=(I_j/100)^2 and Tj=j/365T_j=j/365 under the approximate calendar convention. Total variance is wj=Tjvjw_j=T_jv_j. The implied forward-variance proxy is

v30,90=T90v90−T30v30T90−T30.v_{30,90}=\frac{T_{90}v_{90}-T_{30}v_{30}}{T_{90}-T_{30}}.
Verify the provider horizons and definitions; raw index differences are not forward variance. Flag negative proxies rather than automatically repairing them. Finite option strikes and index construction qualify the risk-neutral interpretation.

Physical targets and information sets

For daily log returns udu_d and origin tt, annualize the sum over audited trading sessions inside the next hh calendar days:

Yt,h=365h∑t<d≤t+hud2.Y_{t,h}=\frac{365}{h}\sum_{t<d\le t+h}u_d^2.
A missing required session makes the target missing. The outcome becomes available no earlier than t+ht+h. Build historical-variance, level-only and level-plus-slope models with positive variance predictions, using only matured training targets. Use the same held-out origins and horizon for QLIKE logf+Y/f\log f+Y/f and squared-error comparisons; overlapping targets require dependence-aware inference. French market returns are a broad-market proxy, not a matched SPX return. Forecast accuracy does not imply equality between ℙ\mathbb P and ℚ\mathbb Q variance expectations.

Research checks

Check common month-end dates and complete 30/90-calendar-day target intervals before fitting. Preserve the information set at every forecast origin; separate the implied level and forward proxy, and score common outcomes.
