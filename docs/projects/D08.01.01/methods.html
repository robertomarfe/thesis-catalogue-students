Variance Swap Pricing and Discrete Monitoring Bias

Research question

How large is the discrete-monitoring correction to variance-swap fair strikes in diffusion and jump-diffusion models, and how much of the difference from log-contract replication is due to jumps rather than discretization?

Moment decomposition

At zero carry set Xh=bh+σhZ+∑k=1NhJkX_h=bh+\sigma\sqrt h Z+\sum_{k=1}^{N_h}J_k, with Poisson intensity λh\lambda h and martingale drift b=−σ2/2−λ(𝔼eJ−1)b=-\sigma^2/2-\lambda(\mathbb E e^J-1). Put m=b+λ𝔼Jm=b+\lambda\mathbb E J and v=σ2+λ𝔼J2v=\sigma^2+\lambda\mathbb E J^2. Then 𝔼Xh2=vh+m2h2\mathbb E X_h^2=vh+m^2h^2. Uniform sampling gives annualized monitoring correction m2hm^2h; expected continuous quadratic variation per year is vv.

Log contract

The diffusion log-contract quantity −2𝔼log(ST/S0)/T-2\mathbb E\log(S_T/S_0)/T becomes σ2+2λ𝔼(eJ−1−J)\sigma^2+2\lambda\mathbb E(e^J-1-J) under jumps, not generally vv. The static option-strip representation uses weights proportional to 2/K22/K^2 plus linear and cash terms. Quantify truncation separately. None of these variance strikes is the fair volatility-swap strike.

Research checks

Verify the diffusion limit and jump moments, isolate monitoring, jump and option-strip errors, and report MC intervals even if a small correction is unresolved.
