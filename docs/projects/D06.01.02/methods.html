Barrier Options and Discrete Monitoring Risk

How large is discrete-grid bias in pricing a continuously monitored down-and-out call, and how effectively can a Brownian bridge remove it? How does the economic price difference for a genuinely discretely monitored contract vary with barrier proximity?

Two contracts and one path law

For lower barrier B<S0B<S_0, strike KK and maturity TT, the continuously monitored payoff is (ST−K)+𝟏{inf0≤t≤TSt>B}(S_T-K)^+\mathbf1\{\inf_{0\le t\le T}S_t>B\}. Discrete monitoring instead checks agreed dates including expiry. Conditional on log endpoints, crossing probability does not depend on drift. For x,y>b=logBx,y>b=\log B, interval survival is p(x,y;h)=1−exp[−2(x−b)(y−b)/(σ2h)].p(x,y;h)=1-\exp[-2(x-b)(y-b)/(\sigma^2h)]. It is zero if either endpoint is at or below bb. Multiply conditional interval survivals or sample crossings, using stable evaluation near the boundary. Use a reflected transition density for an independent continuous reference within its domain.

Numerical versus economic difference

On common endpoints the discrete payoff weakly exceeds the conditional continuous payoff. This is a monitoring premium between distinct contracts, or discretization bias only when the target is continuous monitoring. Check in/out parity with identical monitoring and the low-barrier vanilla limit.
