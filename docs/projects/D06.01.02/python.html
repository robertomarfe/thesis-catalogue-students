Goal

Barrier prices depend on whether a threshold is observed continuously or only at specified dates. A discrete simulation can reproduce one contract exactly while approximating another badly. Near the barrier, unobserved crossings make this economic distinction particularly important.

Research question: How large is discrete-grid bias in pricing a continuously monitored down-and-out call, and how effectively can a Brownian bridge remove it? How does the economic price difference for a genuinely discretely monitored contract vary with barrier proximity?

Student-owned research

Use paired SE on identical endpoints within each grid; 12/52/252 are not nested. Do not bridge-adjust a genuine discrete contract. Include the immediate-knockout case and retain underflow diagnostics.

  1. Implement vanilla and barrier payoff; validate limiting cases and knock-in/out parity.

  2. Reproduce analytic continuous down-and-out prices for B<K and B<S0; confirm formula domain.

  3. Compare naive grid, bridge-weighted estimates and analytic benchmark with paired MC confidence intervals; assess bias, RMSE and computation cost for barrier and grid changes.

  4. Interpret the grid-contract premium difference and demonstrate whether bridge estimator residual errors are statistically consistent with zero.

Minimum viable thesis: One continuous down-and-out call type, analytical validation, three grids and barriers, MC uncertainty and clear distinction between simulation error and contractual monitoring.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
