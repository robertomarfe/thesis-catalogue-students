Modeling Commodity Spot Prices with Mean-Reverting Processes

Does a mean-reverting log-spot model improve out-of-sample WTI monthly price forecasts relative to a random walk, and is any gain robust to horizon, regime and estimation window?

Exact transition and admissibility

For monthly interval h=1/12h=1/12 years, the OU transition is Gaussian with conditional mean μ+(xt−μ)e−κh\mu+(x_t-\mu)e^{-\kappa h} and variance σ2(1−e−2κh)/(2κ)\sigma^2(1-e^{-2\kappa h})/(2\kappa). The discrete AR(1) coefficient is ϕ=e−κh\phi=e^{-\kappa h}; only 0<ϕ<10<\phi<1 maps to positive finite mean reversion. Report inadmissible fits rather than clipping silently. Distinguish conditional log forecast from mean price forecast (which requires the lognormal variance correction under the model).

Release lag and forecast comparisons

At end of month tt, if the latest available monthly average is t−1t-1, the target for month t+Ht+H requires H+1H+1 transitions, for OU and random walk alike. Verify actual release rules. Do not compress missing months or take logs of nonpositive prices; report exceptions and a level-model sensitivity where necessary. Test on common origins and target dates; overlap requires block or HAC inference. Physical estimates are not risk-neutral pricing parameters.

Research checks

Audit positivity, complete monthly dates, release lag and actual targets. Verify OU transition and random-walk limit on artificial data, report inadmissible fits and paired loss comparisons.
