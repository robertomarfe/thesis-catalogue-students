Inflation Risk and the Cross-Section of Equity Returns

Which industry and characteristic portfolios have the strongest nominal and real return exposure to unexpected CPI inflation, and is the cross-sectional relation between inflation beta and average excess return robust to market beta?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

With quarterly average CPI IqI_q, let πq=Iq/Iq−1−1\pi_q=I_q/I_{q-1}-1 and π̂q=(1+fq−1/100)1/4−1\widehat\pi_q=(1+f_{q-1}/100)^{1/4}-1. The surprise is uq=πq−π̂qu_q=\pi_q-\widehat\pi_q.

Here fq−1f_{q-1} is the previous-vintage annualized quarterly-average CPI forecast. Verify target and vintage, not just column names. For nominal gross portfolio and bill returns the real simple excess return is (Ri−Rb)/(1+πq)(R_i-R_b)/(1+\pi_q), not nominal excess minus inflation. Exposure regressions include a consistently defined market excess return and the same surprise. Regress average excess returns on estimated inflation and market betas, reporting rank and leverage. Re-estimate both stages jointly in calendar-quarter blocks to preserve cross-asset dependence and generated-beta uncertainty. The relation is descriptive, not a structural risk price.

Research checks

Hand-check one quarterly-average CPI realization and one forecast error from the prior vintage; compound market, portfolio and bill legs separately. Estimate nominal and real exposures on matched quarters and jointly bootstrap quarters, re-estimating both stages. Report common sample, rank, regime split and revised-CPI caveat.
