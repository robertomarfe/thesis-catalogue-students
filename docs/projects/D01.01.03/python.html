D01.01.03 | Student research starter

Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

Vanilla options reveal distributional information beyond mean and variance because their payoffs are nonlinear. A controlled terminal-distribution experiment can isolate how asymmetry and tail thickness affect the smile without confounding the comparison with a different forward or log-return variance. The task is not to recover a unique distribution from its first four moments.

Research question: How do risk-neutral skewness and excess kurtosis change European option prices and implied volatility smiles when forward and log variance are matched?

Setup check

Check weights and central-moment units on an elementary discrete distribution, not a lognormal mixture. This artificial example does not validate the full research design or actual data.

import numpy as np

print('Setup check passed.')

Student-owned research

Match log variance and forward before reporting skew or kurtosis. Include a symmetric variance mixture, an asymmetric location mixture and its mirror. Use the same strike coordinates and display the actual moments alongside the smile; the arithmetic variance match is a robustness exercise.

  1. Compute analytic mean, variance, skewness and excess kurtosis; verify forward equality and put-call parity.

  2. Compare call/put prices and invert BSM implied vol under arbitrage bounds.

  3. Relate skew direction to IV skew and symmetric heavy tails to wings, holding log variance fixed.

  4. Vary mixtures with same first two moments; attempt matching skew/kurtosis numerically with alternative mixtures to illustrate remaining nonidentification.

  5. Check MC precision, strike-grid convexity and sensitivity to matching terminal simple-return variance instead of log variance.

Minimum viable thesis: At one maturity: Gaussian and at least three mixture cases, exact forward/log variance matches, analytic pricing, no-arbitrage checks and smile comparisons. Distinct from D01.01.01 sensitivities and D01.01.02 dynamic hedging.
