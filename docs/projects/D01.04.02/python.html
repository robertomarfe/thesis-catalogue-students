Current review draft; approval pending. Synthetic core; no mandatory external data.

Goal

Short-dated option skew can be steep even when the expected variance curve is smooth. Rough volatility changes the sensitivity of instantaneous variance to recent innovations. The scientific challenge is to separate the predicted short-time scaling from the numerical error in pricing options at very short expiries.

Research question: How does the roughness parameter alter the scaling of near-ATM skew as maturity decreases, and can the predicted distinction be measured reliably with numerical prices?

Student-owned research

Validate the covariance and cross-covariance before simulating prices. Keep the initial variance curve and other parameters fixed across H. Separate grid refinement, sampling uncertainty and finite-maturity asymptotic bias, with a Brownian-kernel reference.

  1. Verify Gaussian kernel covariance and E[v_t]=xi0(t), H=.5 limit.

  2. Price short-dated calls using common random numbers and independent finer-grid checks.

  3. Recover IV and paired finite-difference ATM skew with MC uncertainty.

  4. Estimate log absolute skew versus log T over predeclared short-maturity subsets; compare H-.5 as asymptotic prediction, not finite-T equality.

  5. Study eta/rho, grid refinement and strike-window sensitivity.

Minimum viable thesis: Two H values including .5, validated Gaussian kernel, 4+ maturities with error bars, short-skew scaling and convergence caveats.

Record assumptions, independent checks, uncertainty and limitations in a separate research notebook. No core solver or research output is supplied.
