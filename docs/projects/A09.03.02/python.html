Goal

Carry signals differ across bonds, commodities, currencies and other asset classes. A diversified carry composite can reveal whether these sleeves share risk-off exposure, but comparisons depend on the original strategy leverage and return conventions.

Research question: How much common risk is present in published cross-asset carry returns, and does combining the asset-class sleeves reduce downside risk during stress?

Student-owned research

Confirm original workbook, documented units, at least three distinct sleeves, no composites or silent splicing. If the core is unavailable, defer. Record stress proxy timing, notional and common dates.

  1. Inspect workbook labels, distinguish asset-class carry sleeves from global factor, confirm currency and scaling.

  2. Compare means, volatility, skewness, correlations and PCA on common dates, without averaging missing sleeves silently.

  3. Construct equal-weight and trailing inverse-volatility sleeve composites with fixed allocation rules; assess risk-adjusted returns and drawdowns.

  4. Evaluate joint loss frequencies and conditional correlations in NBER recessions or lagged stress regimes; block-bootstrap dates jointly.

  5. Test scaling, sample changes and exclusion of option sleeve; compare updated international/commodity histories without assuming identical methodology.

Minimum viable thesis: Original workbook with at least three distinct asset-class sleeves, common-sample correlation and diversified composite, paired stress analysis; defer if file unavailable. Distinct from currency-only A09.02.02 and multi-asset trend-following A09.04.01.
