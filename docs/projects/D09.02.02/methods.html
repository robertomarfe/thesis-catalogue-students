Credit Default Swap Pricing and Recovery Risk

Premium and protection legs

Define survival Q(t)=exp[−∫0tλ(u)du]Q(t)=\exp[-\int_0^t\lambda(u)du] and discount D(t)D(t). With scheduled accrual αj=tj−tj−1\alpha_j=t_j-t_{j-1} and accrued premium paid at default,

ℒ=∫0TDλQdu,\mathcal L=\int_0^T D\lambda Q\,du,
𝒜=∑jαjD(tj)Q(tj)+∑j∫tj−1tj(u−tj−1)D(u)λ(u)Q(u)du.\mathcal A=\sum_j\alpha_j D(t_j)Q(t_j)+\sum_j\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_j}(u-t_{j-1})D(u)\lambda(u)Q(u)du.
For unit notional, s=(1−R)ℒ/𝒜s=(1-R)\mathcal L/\mathcal A; the buyer’s value at contractual coupon cc is (s−c)𝒜(s-c)\mathcal A on the specified valuation date and remaining schedule. Seller value reverses sign.

Two recovery experiments

At fixed hazard, ∂V/∂R=−ℒ\partial V/\partial R=-\mathcal L. At fixed quote, solve anew for the admissible hazard; both risky annuity and protection leg change. Compare values of the existing contract with coupon and remaining dates unchanged. The par contract has zero value in its own calibration; an off-market contract need not. No upfront convention or ISDA production implementation is assumed.

Research checks

Check zero par MTM, buyer/seller antisymmetry and alternate-recovery repricing. In the continuous-premium flat-hazard limit, s=(1−R)λs=(1-R)\lambda; quarterly accrual is evaluated using its own convention.
