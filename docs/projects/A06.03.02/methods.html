Growth versus Value: Cash Flow Timing and Discount Rate Risk

How much greater is discount-rate sensitivity for stylized growth than value claims, and is that prediction consistent with descriptive portfolio evidence?

Purpose. Understand the theoretical restrictions and plan an independent implementation. This note supplies derivations and conventions, not solved research results.

Mathematical framework

For fixed positive cash flows cjc_j and continuously compounded rate yy, P(y)=∑j=1Hcje−jyP(y)=\sum_{j=1}^H c_j e^{-jy}.

With wj=cje−jy/Pw_j=c_j e^{-jy}/P, duration is −Py/P=∑jjwj-P_y/P=\sum_j j w_j and convexity is Pyy/P=∑jj2wjP_{yy}/P=\sum_j j^2 w_j. Under a maturity-dependent shift, first-order log price change is −∑jjwjΔyj-\sum_j j w_j\Delta y_j. Compare central differences at several step sizes and reprice explicitly when cash flows change. Common initial price does not imply common duration. Historical rate beta incorporates revisions to cash flows and is not this derivative.

Research checks

Normalize two positive cash-flow profiles to identical PV, confirm weights sum to one and analytic duration/convexity via central differences at multiple step sizes. Compare parallel/nonparallel shifts and explicit changed cash-flow scenario; test 50/100y truncation. Historical evidence is optional; no claim about observed growth/value beta without matched data.
