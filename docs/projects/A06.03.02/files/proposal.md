## Motivation

Cash-flow timing produces a transparent difference in discount-rate exposure even when two claims have identical initial market value. An offsetting dividend revision separates a mechanical duration effect from a return response.

## Research question

How much greater is discount-rate sensitivity for stylized growth than value claims, and is that prediction consistent with descriptive portfolio evidence?

## Economic mechanism

For fixed positive cash flows $c_j$ and continuously compounded rate $y$, $P(y)=\sum_{j=1}^H c_j e^{-jy}$.

## Research design

Prescribed growth/value positive profiles normalized at 8% continuously compounded rate; 50/100-year truncation, parallel/nonparallel changes and growth-news offset.

Normalize two positive cash-flow profiles to identical PV, confirm weights sum to one and analytic duration/convexity via central differences at multiple step sizes. Compare parallel/nonparallel shifts and explicit changed cash-flow scenario; test 50/100y truncation. Historical evidence is optional; no claim about observed growth/value beta without matched data.

## Minimum viable thesis

Two matched-price cash-flow profiles, analytic/numerical sensitivity and offsetting-news experiment. Historical evidence is optional S-class extension, explicitly distinguished from theory. Distinct from A06.01.01 valuation ratios and A06.02.01 strip data.

## Scope and feasibility

Stylized DDM is not estimated firm cash flow or equity strip data. Treasury yield changes are not pure discount-rate shocks; growth news and risk premia may co-move. High/low BM portfolios are proxies for investment styles. Avoid treating rate beta as structural duration.

**Prerequisites:** Discounted cash flow, duration, numerical derivatives, regression for optional evidence.

**Optional extension:** Use GKK model-implied style equity curves to compare cash-flow timing under additional assumptions.
